# English Grammar, Syntax, Vocabulary, and Sentence Correction

## 1. Parts of Speech and Sentence Structure
- **Subject-Verb Agreement**:
  - A singular subject requires a singular verb: *"The student writes neatly."*
  - A plural subject requires a plural verb: *"The students write neatly."*
  - Collective nouns: Singular when acting as a unified unit (*"The jury has reached its verdict"*), plural when acting individually (*"The jury are divided in their opinions"*).
  - Indefinite pronouns: *Everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, each, either, neither* take singular verbs (*"Each of the participants was given a certificate"*).
- **Tenses and Aspect**:
  - Simple Present: Habitual actions or universal scientific truths (*"Water boils at 100°C"*). Third-person singular requires `-s` or `-es` (*"He goes to school"*, NOT *"he go"*).
  - Present Continuous: Ongoing action at the moment of speaking (*"She is studying"*).
  - Present Perfect: Completed action with present relevance (*"I have finished my homework"*).
  - Past Perfect: Action completed prior to another past event (*"The train had already left when we reached the station"*).

## 2. Active and Passive Voice, Direct and Indirect Speech
- **Voice Transformation**:
  - Active: Subject performs the action: *"The engineer designed the algorithm."*
  - Passive: Subject receives the action ($O + \text{be} + V_3 + \text{by} + S$): *"The algorithm was designed by the engineer."*
- **Reported (Indirect) Speech**:
  - Tense shifts one step back when the reporting verb is in the past tense:
    Direct: *He said, "I am learning Python."*
    Indirect: *He said that he was learning Python.*

## 3. Common Grammatical Pitfalls and Correction Patterns
1. **Dangling Modifiers**:
   - Incorrect: *"Walking in the park, the trees looked beautiful."*
   - Correct: *"While I was walking in the park, the trees looked beautiful."*
2. **Subject-Verb Distance**:
   - Incorrect: *"The quality of the apples are poor."*
   - Correct: *"The quality of the apples is poor."* (The subject is the singular noun *quality*, not *apples*).
3. **Double Negatives**:
   - Incorrect: *"She didn't do nothing."*
   - Correct: *"She didn't do anything"* or *"She did nothing."*
