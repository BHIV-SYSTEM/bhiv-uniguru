# Physical Geography, Earth Systems, Climate, and World Continents

## 1. Earth's Interior Structure and Plate Tectonics
The Earth is composed of concentric compositional layers:
- **Crust**: Thin outer silicate shell (continental crust: granitic, $\sim 30\text{-}50\text{ km}$ thick; oceanic crust: basaltic, $\sim 5\text{-}10\text{ km}$ thick).
- **Mantle**: Extends to $2,900\text{ km}$ depth; composed of dense peridotite rock. The upper semi-molten ductile layer is the **Asthenosphere**.
- **Core**: Liquid outer core ($2,900\text{ - }5,150\text{ km}$, generating Earth's geomagnetic field via geodynamo convection) and solid iron-nickel inner core ($5,150\text{ - }6,371\text{ km}$).
- **Plate Tectonics (Alfred Wegener's Continental Drift modernized)**:
  Lithospheric plates float on the asthenosphere, creating three main types of plate boundaries:
  1. **Convergent Boundaries**: Plates collide; oceanic crust subducts under continental crust (e.g. Pacific Ring of Fire), or two continental plates collide forming fold mountains (e.g. Indo-Australian plate colliding with Eurasian plate forming the Himalayas).
  2. **Divergent Boundaries**: Plates pull apart, creating new oceanic crust at mid-ocean ridges (e.g. Mid-Atlantic Ridge) or continental rift valleys.
  3. **Transform Boundaries**: Plates slide past horizontally (e.g. San Andreas Fault, California), causing shallow-focus earthquakes.

## 2. Atmosphere, Atmospheric Circulation, and Köppen Climate Classification
- **Atmospheric Layers**:
  1. **Troposphere** ($0\text{ - }12\text{ km}$): Contains 75% of atmospheric mass; temperature decreases with altitude (environmental lapse rate $\sim 6.5^\circ\text{C/km}$); all weather phenomena occur here.
  2. **Stratosphere** ($12\text{ - }50\text{ km}$): Contains the **Ozone Layer** ($O_3$) absorbing harmful solar UV radiation; temperature increases with altitude.
  3. **Mesosphere** ($50\text{ - }85\text{ km}$): Coldest atmospheric layer (down to $-90^\circ\text{C}$); meteors burn up here.
  4. **Thermosphere & Exosphere** ($85\text{+ km}$): High ionization (Ionosphere reflecting radio waves), Auroras occur here.
- **Global Wind Belts & Pressure Cells**:
  Driven by solar heating differential and the **Coriolis Effect** (deflection to the right in Northern Hemisphere, to the left in Southern Hemisphere):
  - Hadley Cells, Ferrel Cells, Polar Cells.
  - Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ), Trade Winds, Westerlies, Polar Easterlies.

## 3. World Continents, Oceans, and Major Physical Features
- **Seven Continents (by land area)**:
  1. Asia ($44.58\text{ million km}^2$) - Highest peak: Mount Everest ($8,848.86\text{ m}$).
  2. Africa ($30.37\text{ million km}^2$) - Longest river: Nile River ($6,650\text{ km}$); largest desert: Sahara.
  3. North America ($24.71\text{ million km}^2$) - Rocky Mountains, Mississippi River.
  4. South America ($17.84\text{ million km}^2$) - Andes Mountains, Amazon River (largest by discharge).
  5. Antarctica ($14.2\text{ million km}^2$) - Covered by permanent ice sheet.
  6. Europe ($10.18\text{ million km}^2$) - Alps, Volga River.
  7. Australia / Oceania ($8.6\text{ million km}^2$) - Great Barrier Reef, Outback.
- **Five Oceans**: Pacific Ocean (largest & deepest; Mariana Trench at $11,034\text{ m}$), Atlantic Ocean, Indian Ocean, Southern Ocean, Arctic Ocean.
