# Geography of India, River Systems, Climate, and Agriculture

## 1. Physiographic Divisions of India
India covers an area of $3.287\text{ million km}^2$ with six distinct physiographic divisions:
1. **The Northern Himalayan Mountains**:
   - Geologically young fold mountains extending over $2,400\text{ km}$ from the Indus gorge to the Brahmaputra gorge.
   - Three parallel ranges:
     - **Himadri (Greater Himalayas)**: Average altitude $6,000\text{ m}$; contains Kanchenjunga ($8,586\text{ m}$, highest peak in India).
     - **Himachal (Lesser Himalayas)**: Altitude $3,700\text{-}4,500\text{ m}$; hill stations (Shimla, Manali).
     - **Shiwalik (Outer Himalayas)**: Altitude $900\text{-}1,100\text{ m}$; composed of unconsolidated sediments.
2. **The Northern Great Plains**:
   - Formed by the alluvial deposits of the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra river systems.
   - Subdivisions: Bhabar (pebble zone), Terai (marshy belt), Bhangar (older alluvium), Khadar (newer fertile flood plain alluvium).
3. **The Peninsular Plateau**:
   - Ancient Gondwana shield consisting of the Central Highlands (Malwa, Chota Nagpur) and Deccan Plateau, bounded by the Western Ghats (Sahyadri, continuous with passes) and Eastern Ghats (discontinuous, eroded).
4. **The Indian Desert (Thar Desert)**: Arid region west of the Aravalli Range; river Luni is the principal drainage.
5. **The Coastal Plains**: Western Coastal Plain (narrow; Konkan, Malabar) and Eastern Coastal Plain (wide, deltaic; Coromandel, Northern Circars).
6. **The Islands**: Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal (volcanic origin; Barren Island) and Lakshadweep Islands in the Arabian Sea (coral atolls).

## 2. Indian Drainage & River Systems
- **Himalayan Rivers (Perennial, snow-fed)**:
  - **Indus River System**: Originates near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet; major tributaries: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj.
  - **Ganga River System**: Originates at Gangotri Glacier as Bhagirathi; meets Alaknanda at Devprayag to become the Ganga. Right bank tributaries: Yamuna (largest tributary, meets at Prayagraj), Son. Left bank tributaries: Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi. Enters Bangladesh as Padma, forming the Sundarbans Delta with the Brahmaputra.
  - **Brahmaputra River System**: Originates in Tibet as Yarlung Tsangpo; enters Arunachal Pradesh through Dihang gorge; forms Majuli Island (largest river island).
- **Peninsular Rivers (Rain-fed, seasonal)**:
  - East-flowing rivers into the Bay of Bengal: **Godavari** (longest peninsular river, *"Dakshin Ganga"*), **Krishna**, **Cauvery (Kaveri)**, **Mahanadi**.
  - West-flowing rivers into the Arabian Sea through rift valleys: **Narmada** and **Tapi (Tapti)** (form estuaries, not deltas).

## 3. Climate, Monsoons, and Agriculture in India
- **The Indian Monsoon**:
  - Southwest Monsoon (June - September): Driven by differential heating of land and sea, low pressure over northwestern India, and the shifting of the ITCZ, bringing 75%+ of annual precipitation.
  - Retreating / Northeast Monsoon (October - November): Brings winter rainfall to coastal Tamil Nadu.
- **Cropping Seasons**:
  1. **Kharif Season** (Sown with monsoon onset in June, harvested Sept-Oct): Rice, maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, soybean.
  2. **Rabi Season** (Sown in winter Oct-Dec, harvested April-June): Wheat, barley, mustard, gram, peas.
  3. **Zaid Season** (Short summer season between Rabi and Kharif): Watermelon, cucumber, seasonal vegetables, fodder.
