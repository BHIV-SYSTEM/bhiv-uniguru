# Physical Chemistry, Electrochemistry, and Biochemistry

## 1. Chemical Kinetics and Equilibrium
- **Rate Law**: The relationship between reactant concentrations and reaction rate:
  $$\text{Rate} = k [A]^m [B]^n$$
  where $m$ and $n$ are experimental reaction orders, and $k$ is the rate constant governed by the **Arrhenius Equation**:
  $$k = A \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{R T}\right)$$
  ($E_a$ is activation energy, $R$ is universal gas constant $8.314\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$).
- **Chemical Equilibrium**:
  For $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$:
  $$K_{\text{eq}} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$
- **Le Chatelier's Principle**: If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing conditions (temperature, pressure, concentration), the position of equilibrium shifts to counteract the disturbance.

## 2. Thermochemistry and Electrochemistry
- **Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G$)**: Determines reaction spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure:
  $$\Delta G = \Delta H - T \Delta S$$
  - $\Delta G < 0$: Spontaneous process (exergonic).
  - $\Delta G = 0$: System at chemical equilibrium.
  - $\Delta G > 0$: Non-spontaneous process (endergonic).
- **Nernst Equation**: Relates reduction potential of an electrochemical reaction to standard electrode potential $E^\circ$ and reaction quotient $Q$:
  $$E = E^\circ - \frac{R T}{n F} \ln(Q) \approx E^\circ - \frac{0.0591}{n} \log_{10}(Q) \quad \text{at } 298\text{ K}$$
  ($F = 96485\text{ C/mol}$ is Faraday's constant).

## 3. Biochemistry Fundamentals
- **Proteins and Enzymes**: Polypeptide chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds ($-CO-NH-$). Enzymes act as biological catalysts lowering activation energy $E_a$ without altering equilibrium $K_{\text{eq}}$.
- **Nucleic Acids (DNA and RNA)**:
  - DNA consists of two antiparallel polynucleotide chains in a right-handed double helix.
  - Nitrogenous bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C).
  - Watson-Crick base pairing: A pairs with T via 2 hydrogen bonds ($A=T$); G pairs with C via 3 hydrogen bonds ($G\equiv C$).
- **Cellular Respiration**:
  Aerobic oxidation of glucose yielding ATP:
  $$C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \to 6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \sim 30\text{-}32\text{ ATP}$$
