# The Four Vedas, Principal Upanishads, and Vedic Sciences

## 1. The Four Canonical Vedas (Chatur-Veda)
The Vedas are the foundational shruti (revealed) texts of Indian civilizational thought, preserved over millennia through sophisticated oral mnemonic chanting (Padapatha, Kramapatha, Jatapatha, Ghanapatha):
1. **Rigveda (Ṛgveda)**:
   - The oldest extant Indo-European literary composition, consisting of 1,028 hymns (suktas) organized into 10 Mandalas.
   - Contains hymns dedicated to cosmic principles: Agni (fire/transformation), Indra (strength/rain), Varuna (moral order/Rta), Soma, and Ushas (dawn).
   - Core philosophical hymns:
     - **Nasadiya Sukta** (Rigveda 10.129): Profound hymn of creation exploring cosmic origins beyond being and non-being.
     - **Purusha Sukta** (Rigveda 10.90): Interconnectedness of the cosmos through a universal cosmic being.
     - **Gayatri Mantra** (Rigveda 3.62.10): Composed by Rishi Vishvamitra, dedicated to Savitr (divine illumination).
2. **Samaveda (Sāmaveda)**:
   - The Veda of liturgical melody, chants, and musical notation (*sāman*).
   - Serves as the historical foundation of Indian classical music (Raga and Svara systems).
3. **Yajurveda (Yajurveda)**:
   - Prose mantras and liturgical formulas for rituals and sacrifices.
   - Divided into two primary recensions:
     - **Krishna Yajurveda** (Black/unarranged, e.g., Taittiriya Samhita).
     - **Shukla Yajurveda** (White/arranged, e.g., Vajasaneyi Samhita, containing the Isha Upanishad as its 40th chapter).
4. **Atharvaveda (Atharvaveda)**:
   - Practical, scientific, and societal hymns compiled by sages Atharvan and Angiras.
   - Covers indigenous medicine, herbology, governance, harmony, peace (Shanti hymns), and environmental respect (Prithvi Sukta / Hymn to the Earth).

## 2. Structure of Each Veda & The Principal Upanishads
Every Veda consists of four chronological/conceptual layers:
1. **Samhitas**: Collections of poetic hymns and mantras.
2. **Brahmanas**: Prose texts detailing ritual performance, symbolic meanings, and context.
3. **Aranyakas**: "Forest treatises" transitioning from external ritualism to internal contemplation.
4. **Upanishads**: Philosophical culmination (Vedanta / end of the Vedas) addressing metaphysics, consciousness, and liberation.

### The Ten Principal Upanishads (Dashopanishad commented on by Adi Shankara):
1. **Isha Upanishad**: Unity of the divine in all creation (*"Isha vasyam idam sarvam"*), balance of action and detachment.
2. **Kena Upanishad**: Investigates the true impeller behind human sensory perception and thought (*"By whom impelled flies the mind?"*).
3. **Katha Upanishad**: Dialogue between young seeker Nachiketa and Yama (death) regarding the immortal Self (Atman) and the allegory of the chariot.
4. **Prashna Upanishad**: Answers six cosmological and physiological questions posed by six seeking rishis.
5. **Mundaka Upanishad**: Distinguishes between lower empirical knowledge (*Apara Vidya*) and higher transcendental knowledge (*Para Vidya*); contains India's national motto: *"Satyameva Jayate"* (Truth alone triumphs).
6. **Mandukya Upanishad**: Analyzes human consciousness through the syllable OM across the 4 states: Waking (*Jagrat*), Dreaming (*Svapna*), Deep Sleep (*Sushupti*), and Pure Consciousness (*Turiya*).
7. **Taittiriya Upanishad**: Expounds the **Pancha Kosha** (five sheaths of human personality: Annamaya, Pranamaya, Manomaya, Vijnanamaya, Anandamaya).
8. **Aitareya Upanishad**: Origin of the universe and consciousness; contains Mahavakya *"Prajnanam Brahma"* (Consciousness is Brahman).
9. **Chandogya Upanishad**: Dialogues of Uddalaka Aruni and Shvetaketu; contains Mahavakya *"Tat Tvam Asi"* (Thou art That).
10. **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad**: The largest Upanishad; profound debates of Sage Yajnavalkya at King Janaka's court, Maitreyi dialogue, and Mahavakya *"Aham Brahmasmi"* (I am Brahman).

## 3. Vedangas & Vedic Sciences
To properly understand and apply the Vedas, six auxiliary disciplines (**Vedangas**) were developed:
1. **Shiksha**: Phonetics and correct pronunciation of vowels and consonants.
2. **Chandas**: Poetic meter and rhythm (Gayatri, Trishtubh, Anushtubh).
3. **Vyakarana**: Scientific grammar, codified by **Panini** in the *Ashtadhyayi* (a 4,000-rule generative formal grammar, comparable to modern computer compiler grammars).
4. **Nirukta**: Etymology and semantic lexicography (compiled by Yaska).
5. **Jyotisha**: Astronomy and calendar calculation for planetary alignments and seasons.
6. **Kalpa**: Geometry and practical application (including **Sulba Sutras** by Baudhayana and Apastamba, containing early geometric proofs of the Pythagorean theorem and geometric circle-squaring algorithms).
