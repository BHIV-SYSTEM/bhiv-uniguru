# World History, Global Civilizations, and Landmark Historical Events

## 1. Ancient River Valley Civilizations
Human civilization originated around major river systems facilitating irrigation and agricultural surpluses:
- **Mesopotamian Civilization (Tigris and Euphrates, modern Iraq)**:
  - Sumerians developed Cuneiform writing, the 60-base sexagesimal numbering system (giving us 60 seconds in a minute, 360 degrees in a circle), and the wheel.
  - Babylonians: Code of Hammurabi (one of the earliest written legal codes).
- **Ancient Egyptian Civilization (Nile River)**:
  - Hieroglyphic script, monumental architecture (Pyramids of Giza), papyrus paper, unified kingdom under Pharaohs.
- **Ancient Greece (Hellas)**:
  - Birthplace of Western philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), mathematics (Pythagoras, Euclid, Archimedes), and Athenian direct democracy.
- **Roman Empire & Republic**:
  - Roman Law (Twelve Tables, Corpus Juris Civilis), monumental engineering (aqueducts, Colosseum, paved roads), and the Pax Romana.

## 2. Renaissance, Scientific Revolution, and Enlightenment
- **The Renaissance (14th - 17th Century)**:
  - Cultural rebirth starting in Florence, Italy; revival of classical art and humanism (Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo).
  - Johannes Gutenberg invented the movable type printing press ($\sim 1440$), democratizing knowledge dissemination across Europe.
- **The Scientific Revolution**:
  - Heliocentric solar system model by Nicolaus Copernicus and Galileo Galilei.
  - Isaac Newton's *Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687) formulating universal gravitation and the three laws of motion.
- **The Enlightenment (Age of Reason, 18th Century)**:
  - Thinkers like John Locke (natural rights to life, liberty, property), Montesquieu (separation of powers into legislative, executive, judicial), Voltaire, and Rousseau (social contract).

## 3. The Industrial Revolution and World Wars
- **The Industrial Revolution (Late 18th - 19th Century)**:
  - Transition from manual agrarian production to mechanized factory systems driven by James Watt's improved steam engine, textile mechanization, and coal/iron industries.
- **World War I (1914 - 1918)**:
  - Triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo; pitted Allied Powers against Central Powers. Ended with the Treaty of Versailles (1919) and the establishment of the League of Nations.
- **World War II (1939 - 1945)**:
  - Triggered by Nazi Germany's invasion of Poland; Allied Powers (USA, USSR, UK, China) fought against Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, Japan).
  - Ended in 1945 with the unconditional surrender of Axis forces and the atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, leading directly to the founding of the **United Nations (UN)** in October 1945.
- **The Cold War (1947 - 1991)**:
  - Ideological, geopolitical rivalry between Western bloc (USA / NATO) and Eastern bloc (USSR / Warsaw Pact); nuclear arms race, Space Race, ending with the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.
