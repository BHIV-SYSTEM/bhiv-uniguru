# Puranas

## Canonical Name

Puranas

## Sanskrit

पुराण

## Transliteration

Purana (singular); Puranas (plural)

## Literal Meaning

Purana means "ancient" or "that which is old yet ever new." From Sanskrit pura (formerly, of old) + ana (living, breathing).

## Functional Meaning

The Puranas are a genre of ancient Indian literature that preserve cosmology, genealogy, mythology, philosophy, and dharmic teaching in narrative form. They are the primary vehicle through which Vedic and Upanishadic knowledge was transmitted to the general population across all castes and communities.

## Purpose of the Puranas

The Puranas serve five primary purposes (Panchalakshana — the five characteristics of a Purana):

1. **Sarga** — Primary creation: the origin of the universe from Brahman
2. **Pratisarga** — Secondary creation: the cycles of dissolution and re-creation
3. **Vamsha** — Genealogy of gods, sages, and royal dynasties
4. **Manvantara** — The epochs of Manu (cosmic time cycles)
5. **Vamshanucharita** — History of the solar and lunar dynasties

Beyond these five, the Puranas serve as encyclopedias of dharma, devotion (bhakti), cosmology, geography, medicine, astronomy, and ethics — making the Vedic tradition accessible to all.

## Cosmology

The Puranas describe the cyclic cosmology of creation and dissolution. Brahman is the ocean from which the universe arises like waves. The concept of Brahmananda (Brahman's egg) is the cosmic egg from which the universe hatches. The Puranas elaborate the four yugas (Krita, Treta, Dvapara, Kali) and the progressive decline of dharma across cosmic time cycles. In Puranic cosmology, Dharma is personified as a deity standing on four legs in Krita Yuga, declining to one leg in Kali Yuga.

## Relation to Vedas and Upanishads

The Puranas are called the fifth Veda (Panchamaveda). They do not replace the Vedas but make their teachings accessible through story, symbol, and devotion. The Bhagavata Purana is considered the ripened fruit of the Vedic tree. The Puranas preserve and transmit the Upanishadic teachings on Brahman, Atman, karma, dharma, and moksha in narrative form.

## Ayurveda Connection

The Agni Purana contains extensive sections on Ayurveda, including descriptions of medicinal plants, treatments, and the principles of health. The Garuda Purana contains medical knowledge related to the body and death. The Puranas thus served as repositories of practical knowledge including medicine, alongside cosmology and dharma.

## Bhakti and Devotion

The Puranas are the primary scriptural foundation of the bhakti (devotion) traditions. The Bhagavata Purana (especially the tenth book on Krishna) is the central text of Vaishnava bhakti. The Shiva Purana is the primary text of Shaiva devotion. The Devi Bhagavata Purana is central to Shakta traditions.

## Governance

The Puranas preserve the concept of Rajadharma (royal duty) through the genealogies of righteous kings. The ideal ruler is one who upholds dharma, protects the people, and governs according to the cosmic order described in the Puranas.

## Related Concepts

- Dharma (the Puranas are the primary narrative vehicle for dharmic teaching)
- Karma (Puranic stories illustrate the law of karma through narrative)
- Moksha (the Puranas describe the paths to liberation)
- Lokas (the Puranic cosmology of worlds and realms)
- Yugas (the Puranic doctrine of cosmic time cycles)
- Bhakti (the Puranas are the scriptural foundation of devotional traditions)

## Related Shastras

- Vedas (the Puranas are the narrative expansion of Vedic teaching)
- Upanishads (the Puranas transmit Upanishadic philosophy in story form)
- Mahabharata (the Puranas share genealogical and cosmological material)
- Ramayana (the Puranas reference and expand Ramayana narratives)
- Ayurveda (the Agni Purana and Garuda Purana contain medical knowledge)
