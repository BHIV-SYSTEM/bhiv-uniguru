# Ahimsa

## Canonical Name

Ahimsa

## Sanskrit

अहिंसा

## Transliteration

Ahimsa

## Literal Meaning

Non-violence; non-harm; non-injury. From Sanskrit a (not) + himsa (violence, harm, injury).

## Ahimsa Paramo Dharma

The phrase **Ahimsa Paramo Dharma** (अहिंसा परमो धर्मः) means:

**"Non-violence is the highest dharma."**

- Ahimsa = non-violence
- Paramo = highest, supreme
- Dharma = duty, righteousness, cosmic order

This maxim appears in the Mahabharata (Anushasana Parva 115.1) and is one of the most cited ethical statements in Indian tradition. The full verse continues: "Ahimsa paramo dharma, tatha ahimsa paramo tapah" — Non-violence is the highest dharma; non-violence is the highest austerity.

## Functional Meaning

Ahimsa is the principle of not causing harm to any living being — in thought, word, or action. It is the foundational ethical principle shared across Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist traditions. In Jainism, ahimsa is the first and supreme vow. In Yoga, ahimsa is the first of the five Yamas (ethical restraints) in Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga. In the Mahabharata, ahimsa is declared the highest dharma.

## Jain Teaching on Ahimsa

The Acharanga Sutra (the oldest Jain canonical text) states the core teaching of ahimsa:

"All beings desire to live. None wishes to die. Therefore, a wise person does not kill, nor causes others to kill, nor consents to killing."

The Five Great Vows (Mahavrata) for Jain monks place Ahimsa first:
1. Ahimsa — Non-violence toward all living beings
2. Satya — Absolute truthfulness
3. Asteya — Non-stealing
4. Brahmacharya — Celibacy
5. Aparigraha — Complete non-possessiveness

## Yoga Teaching on Ahimsa

In Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (2.30), Ahimsa is the first of the five Yamas:
"Ahimsa-satya-asteya-brahmacharya-aparigraha yamah" — Non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-possessiveness are the five restraints.

Patanjali states (Yoga Sutras 2.35): "Ahimsa-pratishthayam tat-sannidhau vaira-tyagah" — When one is established in non-violence, all enmity is abandoned in one's presence.

## Dharma Connection

The Mahabharata (Anushasana Parva) states: "Ahimsa paramo dharma" — Non-violence is the highest dharma. The Caraka Samhita (Ayurvedic text) states that the physician's svadharma includes ahimsa (non-harm) as the first ethical principle. Dharma.md cross-references Ahimsa as a related concept.

## Cosmology and Ethics

Ahimsa operates at multiple levels:
- Physical: not harming the body of any being
- Verbal: not speaking words that harm
- Mental: not harboring harmful thoughts

In Jain cosmology, every act of violence (himsa) generates karma that binds the soul. Ahimsa is therefore both an ethical principle and a metaphysical necessity for liberation (moksha).
