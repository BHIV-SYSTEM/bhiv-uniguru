# Purusha

## Canonical Name

Purusha

## Sanskrit

पुरुष

## Transliteration

Puruṣa

## Śabda

पुरुष

## Dhātu

पुर् (Pur) — To fill, to precede; also पुरि शेते (Puri śete) — that which lies in the city (the body). Puruṣa is "that which fills" or "that which dwells in the city (body)."

## Vyākaraṇa

Puruṣa is a masculine noun. It has multiple meanings: (1) the cosmic person (Ṛgveda 10.90), (2) the individual soul (Sāṃkhya), (3) the supreme being (Viṣṇu/Brahman), (4) a human being (man).

## Nirukta

Yāska: "puri śayānāt puruṣaḥ" — that which lies in the city (body). The soul dwells in the body as a person dwells in a city.

## Bīja

ॐ (Om) — the primordial sound of Puruṣa. Also सोऽहम् (So'ham) — the natural mantra of Puruṣa (I am That).

## Tattva

Puruṣa-Tattva — the principle of pure consciousness and cosmic personhood. In Sāṃkhya: Puruṣa is one of the two ultimate realities (along with Prakṛti). It is pure consciousness (cit), eternally free, and the witness of Prakṛti's evolution.

## Śakti

Puruṣa is the śaktimān (the possessor of śakti) — Prakṛti is his śakti. Puruṣa himself is pure consciousness without activity — his presence activates Prakṛti.

## Literal Meaning

The cosmic person; the individual soul; the supreme being; a human being.

## Functional Meaning

Puruṣa is the principle of pure consciousness that witnesses Prakṛti's evolution without being affected by it. In Sāṃkhya: Puruṣa is the eternal, unchanging witness — it does not act, does not create, does not suffer. It is the pure light of consciousness in which Prakṛti's evolution appears. Liberation (kaivalya) is Puruṣa's recognition of its eternal distinction from Prakṛti. In the Ṛgveda (10.90), the cosmic Puruṣa is the primordial being from whose sacrifice the universe was created.

## Ontology

In Sāṃkhya: Puruṣa is one of the two ultimate realities — pure consciousness, eternally distinct from Prakṛti. There are infinite individual Puruṣas (unlike Advaita's one Ātman). In Advaita: Puruṣa = Ātman = Brahman — the one universal consciousness. In Viśiṣṭādvaita: Puruṣa is the individual soul (jīva) — real, individual, and eternally part of Brahman-Viṣṇu. In Yoga: Puruṣa is the witness-consciousness (sākṣī) that observes the mind's modifications.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: the Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.90) describes the cosmic Puruṣa as the primordial being with a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet — encompassing the entire universe. From the sacrifice of the cosmic Puruṣa, the universe was created: the moon from his mind, the sun from his eye, Indra and Agni from his mouth, Vāyu from his breath, the sky from his navel, the earth from his feet.

## Psychology

Puruṣa as psychological principle: the witness-consciousness (sākṣī) that observes all mental activity without being affected. The goal of yoga is to identify with Puruṣa (the witness) rather than with Prakṛti (the mind and body). The concept of puruṣa-viveka (discrimination of Puruṣa from Prakṛti) is the central practice of Sāṃkhya-Yoga.

## Governance

The concept of Puruṣa in governance: the ideal ruler is one who governs from the standpoint of Puruṣa — as a witness, without personal attachment. The concept of rāja-puruṣa (the royal person) — the king as the embodiment of the cosmic Puruṣa in the kingdom.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Puruṣa is the conscious principle that animates the body. The concept of puruṣa-prakṛti (the individual's consciousness-nature combination) determines health and disease tendencies. The Caraka Saṃhitā describes the physician's task as restoring the harmony between Puruṣa and Prakṛti in the patient.

## Mathematics

The concept of Puruṣa in mathematics: the concept of the observer in quantum mechanics has been compared to the Sāṃkhya Puruṣa — the observer who does not interfere but whose presence affects the observed. This remains a philosophical analogy.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Puruṣa is associated with the Sun (Sūrya) — the cosmic witness, the source of light. The concept of Kālapuruṣa (the person of time) — the zodiac is mapped onto the body of the cosmic Puruṣa.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: Puruṣa is the gold (suvarṇa) — the pure, unchanging metal that represents pure consciousness. The alchemical goal of transmuting base metals to gold mirrors the spiritual goal of recognizing Puruṣa beneath Prakṛti.

## Ritual

The Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.90) is recited in all major Vedic rituals. The concept of puruṣa-medha (human sacrifice) — a symbolic ritual in which the cosmic Puruṣa's sacrifice is re-enacted. The concept of puruṣa-sūkta-pāṭha (recitation of the Puruṣa Sūkta) as a daily ritual.

## Symbolism

The cosmic person (viśvarūpa) symbolizes Puruṣa — the universe as the body of the cosmic being. The sun symbolizes Puruṣa — the self-luminous witness. The mirror symbolizes Puruṣa — it reflects all without being affected.

## Traditional Interpretations

- Sāṃkhya: Puruṣa is pure consciousness, eternally distinct from Prakṛti; there are infinite individual Puruṣas
- Advaita: Puruṣa = Ātman = Brahman — the one universal consciousness
- Viśiṣṭādvaita: Puruṣa is the individual soul — real, individual, part of Brahman
- Yoga: Puruṣa is the witness-consciousness; Īśvara is a special Puruṣa (untouched by karma)

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Puruṣa as the cosmic person (Puruṣa Sūkta)
- Upaniṣadic period: Puruṣa as the individual soul and the cosmic self
- Sāṃkhya period: Puruṣa systematized as pure consciousness
- Yoga period: Puruṣa as the witness-consciousness; Īśvara as a special Puruṣa
- Bhagavad Gītā: Puruṣottama (the supreme Puruṣa) as the highest reality
