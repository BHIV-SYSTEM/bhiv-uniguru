# Atman

## Canonical Name

Atman

## Sanskrit

आत्मन्

## Transliteration

Ātman

## Śabda

आत्मन्

## Dhātu

अत् (At) — To move constantly; also connected to अन् (An) — to breathe, to live.

## Vyākaraṇa

Ātman is a masculine noun (though it refers to the genderless Self). Derived from the root at (to move) or an (to breathe). The word appears in all cases and is used reflexively ("oneself"). Pāṇini treats ātman as a reflexive pronoun in certain constructions.

## Nirukta

Yāska: "ātmā vyāpnoti iti" — that which pervades. Also: "ātmā aniti prāṇiti" — that which breathes. The Self is both the pervader and the life-breath.

## Bīja

अ (A) — the first letter of the Sanskrit alphabet, representing the unmanifest source. In the Gītā (10.33), Kṛṣṇa declares "akṣarāṇām akāro'smi" — among letters I am the letter A.

## Tattva

Ātma-Tattva — the principle of pure consciousness and selfhood. In Advaita: Ātman is identical with Brahman (Aham Brahmāsmi). In Sāṃkhya: Ātman (Puruṣa) is pure consciousness, eternally distinct from Prakṛti. In Viśiṣṭādvaita: Ātman is real but eternally dependent on and part of Brahman-Viṣṇu.

## Śakti

Cit-Śakti — the power of pure consciousness. Ātman is the seat of cit (consciousness), the second aspect of Sat-Cit-Ānanda (Being-Consciousness-Bliss).

## Literal Meaning

The Self; the soul; the innermost essence of a being.

## Functional Meaning

Ātman is the eternal, unchanging Self underlying individual existence. It is the witness (sākṣī) of all mental and physical activity, never itself modified. The Upaniṣads teach that Ātman is not the body, not the mind, not the intellect, not the ego — it is the pure awareness in which all these appear. The realization of Ātman as identical with Brahman (Aham Brahmāsmi) is the goal of Advaita Vedānta.

## Ontology

In Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkara): Ātman = Brahman; the apparent difference is due to avidyā (ignorance). The mahāvākyas (great sayings) express this identity: "Aham Brahmāsmi," "Tat tvam asi," "Prajñānam Brahma," "Ayam Ātmā Brahma." In Sāṃkhya: Ātman (Puruṣa) is one of infinite individual consciousnesses, eternally distinct from Prakṛti. In Viśiṣṭādvaita: Ātman is real, individual, and eternally a part (aṃśa) of Brahman-Viṣṇu. In Dvaita (Madhva): Ātman is eternally distinct from Brahman; five eternal distinctions (pañca-bheda) are real.

## Cosmology

In Purāṇic cosmology: the individual ātman (jīvātman) is a spark of the universal Paramātman. The ātman transmigrates through the fourteen lokas according to karma. In Sāṃkhya cosmology: Puruṣa (Ātman) is the passive witness; Prakṛti (matter) evolves the universe for Puruṣa's experience and eventual liberation.

## Psychology

Ātman is the deepest layer of the self, beyond the five koshas (sheaths). In Yoga psychology: the ātman is the sākṣī (witness) that observes the modifications of the mind (citta-vṛttis) without being affected. The goal of yoga is to still the mind so the ātman shines in its own nature (svarūpa). The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad describes four states of consciousness (waking, dreaming, deep sleep, turīya) and identifies Ātman with the fourth state (turīya) that underlies all three.

## Governance

The concept of ātman in governance: the ruler who recognizes the ātman in all beings governs with compassion and justice. The Arthaśāstra's concept of rāja-dharma is grounded in the king's recognition of his own ātman and its reflection in all subjects. Aśoka's transformation after Kalinga was described in terms of recognizing the ātman in the suffering of others.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: the ātman is the ultimate cause of life (jīvana-hetu). Disease occurs when the ātman's connection to the body is disturbed. The concept of ātma-jñāna (self-knowledge) is considered the highest medicine. Caraka Saṃhitā states that the physician who knows the ātman can heal at the deepest level.

## Mathematics

The concept of ātman in mathematics: the Upaniṣadic teaching "ekam evādvitīyam" (one without a second) is the mathematical statement of non-duality. The concept of infinity (ananta) in Indian mathematics is connected to the infinite nature of Ātman-Brahman. Zero (śūnya) in Indian mathematics represents the void from which the ātman emerges.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: the Sun (Sūrya) is the cosmic symbol of Ātman — the self-luminous source of all light. The Sun's position in the birth chart (Sūrya-rāśi) represents the ātman's expression in this life. The concept of Sūrya as Ātman appears in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions (Rasaśāstra): gold (suvarṇa) is the metal of the ātman — pure, unchanging, self-luminous. The alchemical goal of transmuting base metals to gold mirrors the spiritual goal of realizing the ātman beneath the base ego.

## Ritual

The Upanayana (sacred thread ceremony) initiates the student into the knowledge of Ātman. The Mahāvākya "Tat tvam asi" is transmitted from teacher to student as the core teaching. The Śrāddha ritual is based on the belief in the ātman's continuity after death. The Ahaṃgrahopāsanā (meditation on "I am Brahman") is the primary ātman-realization practice.

## Symbolism

The flame (dīpa) symbolizes Ātman — self-luminous, unaffected by wind (worldly disturbances). The sky (ākāśa) symbolizes Ātman's all-pervading, uncontained nature. The mirror symbolizes the mind reflecting the ātman's light. The lotus in the heart (hṛdaya-puṇḍarīka) is the seat of the ātman in Upaniṣadic symbolism.

## Related Deities

- Sūrya (the Sun — cosmic symbol of Ātman's self-luminosity)
- Śiva (as Paramātman — the universal Self)
- Viṣṇu (as Paramātman dwelling in all hearts)
- Brahmā (as Hiraṇyagarbha — the cosmic Self)

## Related Lokas

- Brahmaloka (Satyaloka) — the realm where Ātman-Brahman identity is fully realized
- Ātmaloka (the inner realm of self-realization)

## Related Koshas

- Ānandamaya Kosha (bliss sheath) — the innermost sheath, closest to Ātman
- Vijñānamaya Kosha (intellect sheath) — the sheath that can recognize Ātman
- All five koshas are sheaths that veil the Ātman

## Related Chakras

- Sahasrāra (crown chakra) — the seat of Paramātman realization
- Anāhata (heart chakra) — the seat of jīvātman (individual self)
- Ājñā (third eye) — the seat of ātma-vicāra (self-inquiry)

## Related Yantras

- Śrī Yantra (represents the Ātman-Brahman identity in geometric form)
- Bindu (the central point of any yantra — represents the ātman)

## Related Mantras

- "Aham Brahmāsmi" (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10) — I am Brahman
- "Tat tvam asi" (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7) — That thou art
- "Ayam Ātmā Brahma" (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1.2) — This Self is Brahman
- "So'ham" (I am That) — the natural mantra of the breath

## Related Vidyās

- Ātmavidyā (self-knowledge — the highest vidyā)
- Brahmavidyā (knowledge of Brahman = knowledge of Ātman)
- Adhyātma Vidyā (inner science of the Self)

## Related Śāstras

- Upaniṣads (primary source — especially Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya, Māṇḍūkya, Kaṭha, Muṇḍaka)
- Brahma Sūtras (Bādarāyaṇa)
- Bhagavad Gītā (Chapter 2 — Sāṃkhya Yoga; Chapter 13 — Kṣetra-Kṣetrajña)
- Vivekacūḍāmaṇi (Śaṅkara)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkara): Ātman = Brahman; difference is illusory (vivartavāda)
- Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): Ātman is real, individual, and eternally part of Brahman; difference is real (pariṇāmavāda)
- Dvaita (Madhva): Ātman is eternally distinct from Brahman; five eternal differences are real
- Sāṃkhya: Ātman (Puruṣa) is pure consciousness, eternally distinct from Prakṛti
- Buddhism: Anātman (no-self) — the Buddha denied a permanent, unchanging self
- Jainism: Jīva (soul) is real, individual, and capable of liberation; similar to but distinct from Vedic Ātman

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Ātman primarily means "breath" or "life-force"
- Atharvaveda: Ātman begins to acquire the meaning of "self"
- Early Upaniṣads (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya): Ātman = Brahman identity established
- Middle Upaniṣads (Kaṭha, Muṇḍaka): Ātman as the inner controller (antaryāmin)
- Later Upaniṣads: Ātman doctrine fully systematized
- Śaṅkara (8th century CE): Advaita interpretation — Ātman = Brahman
- Rāmānuja (11th century CE): Viśiṣṭādvaita — Ātman as part of Brahman
- Madhva (13th century CE): Dvaita — Ātman eternally distinct from Brahman
