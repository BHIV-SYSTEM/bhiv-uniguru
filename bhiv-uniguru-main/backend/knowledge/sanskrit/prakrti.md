# Prakrti

## Canonical Name

Prakrti

## Sanskrit

प्रकृति

## Transliteration

Prakṛti

## Śabda

प्रकृति

## Dhātu

प्र + कृ (Pra + Kṛ) — Pra (before, primary, forth) + Kṛ (to do, to make). The primary maker; the original nature; that which was made first.

## Vyākaraṇa

Prakṛti is a feminine noun derived from the prefix pra- and the root kṛ (to make) with the suffix -ti. The feminine gender is significant — Prakṛti is the feminine creative principle, complementary to the masculine Puruṣa.

## Nirukta

"Prakṛṣṭā kṛtiḥ prakṛtiḥ" — the primary creation. Prakṛti is the original nature from which all manifest existence emerges.

## Bīja

ह्रीं (Hrīm) — the bīja of Prakṛti as the creative power. Also क्लीं (Klīm) — the bīja of attraction and manifestation.

## Tattva

Prakṛti-Tattva — the principle of primal matter and creative nature. In Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti is one of the two ultimate realities (along with Puruṣa). It is the material cause of the universe — the primal matter from which all manifest existence evolves.

## Śakti

Prakṛti is itself the śakti — the creative power. In Śākta traditions, Prakṛti = Śakti = the Goddess. The three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) are the three śaktis of Prakṛti.

## Literal Meaning

Nature; primal matter; the original creative principle; the feminine creative power.

## Functional Meaning

Prakṛti is the primal, undifferentiated matter from which the entire manifest universe evolves. In Sāṃkhya philosophy, Prakṛti consists of three guṇas (qualities): Sattva (clarity, luminosity), Rajas (activity, passion), Tamas (inertia, darkness). When the three guṇas are in perfect equilibrium, Prakṛti is unmanifest (avyakta). When the equilibrium is disturbed (by Puruṣa's proximity), Prakṛti begins to evolve the universe through twenty-three tattvas (principles).

## Ontology

In Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti is one of the two ultimate realities — it is the material cause of the universe. It is unconscious (acetana) but active. Puruṣa is conscious but inactive. The universe is the product of their interaction. In Advaita: Prakṛti = Māyā — the creative power of Brahman. In Viśiṣṭādvaita: Prakṛti is the body of Brahman-Viṣṇu — real and dependent on Brahman. In Śākta: Prakṛti = the Goddess — the supreme creative reality.

## Cosmology

In Sāṃkhya cosmology: from Prakṛti evolves Mahat (cosmic intellect), then Ahaṃkāra (ego-principle), then the five tanmātras (subtle elements), then the five mahābhūtas (gross elements), then the ten indriyas (sense and action organs), then manas (mind) — twenty-three tattvas in total. The universe is Prakṛti's evolution for Puruṣa's experience and eventual liberation.

## Psychology

Prakṛti as psychological principle: the three guṇas govern all psychological states. Sattva produces clarity, wisdom, and peace. Rajas produces activity, desire, and restlessness. Tamas produces inertia, delusion, and sleep. The goal of yoga is to transcend all three guṇas (triguṇātīta) and rest in Puruṣa's pure consciousness.

## Governance

The concept of Prakṛti in governance: the Arthaśāstra describes the seven elements of the state (saptāṅga) as the Prakṛti of the kingdom — king, minister, territory, fort, treasury, army, ally. The concept of rāja-prakṛti (the nature of the king) — the king's guṇa-composition determines the quality of governance.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Prakṛti is the individual's constitutional nature — the unique combination of the three doṣas (vāta, pitta, kapha) determined at conception. The concept of deha-prakṛti (body-nature) — each person has a unique Prakṛti that determines their health tendencies. Āyurvedic treatment is tailored to the individual's Prakṛti.

## Mathematics

The concept of Prakṛti in mathematics: the three guṇas as the three fundamental operations (addition/sattva, multiplication/rajas, subtraction/tamas). The concept of Prakṛti in Bījaganita (algebra) — the term prakṛti is used for the coefficient in quadratic equations.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: the three guṇas of Prakṛti are expressed in the three types of signs (movable/rajas, fixed/tamas, dual/sattva). The concept of graha-prakṛti (planetary nature) — each planet has its own guṇa composition.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: the three guṇas of Prakṛti are expressed in the three states of matter (solid/tamas, liquid/rajas, gas/sattva). The concept of dhātu-prakṛti (metal nature) — each metal has its own guṇa composition.

## Ritual

The concept of Prakṛti in ritual: the three guṇas determine the nature of ritual offerings. Sattvic offerings (milk, ghee, fruits) are for liberation. Rajasic offerings (meat, wine) are for worldly power. Tamasic offerings are for destructive purposes. The Bhagavad Gītā (17.7-10) describes the three types of food, sacrifice, and worship according to the three guṇas.

## Symbolism

The three guṇas are symbolized by three colors: white (sattva), red (rajas), black (tamas). The three strands of a rope symbolize the three guṇas — together they bind the soul. The Goddess (Devī) symbolizes Prakṛti — the creative, sustaining, and dissolving power.

## Related Deities

- Devī/Mahādevī (Prakṛti as the Goddess — the supreme creative reality)
- Brahmā (the creator who works through Prakṛti)
- Viṣṇu (Prakṛti as his body in Viśiṣṭādvaita)
- Śiva (Prakṛti as his Śakti)

## Related Lokas

- All lokas are within Prakṛti — they are Prakṛti's manifestations
- Beyond Prakṛti is Puruṣa's realm (kaivalya)

## Related Koshas

- All five koshas are manifestations of Prakṛti
- The Ātman (Puruṣa) is beyond Prakṛti

## Related Chakras

- All seven chakras are within Prakṛti
- The awakening of kuṇḍalinī is Prakṛti's śakti rising toward Puruṣa

## Related Yantras

- Śrī Yantra (the geometric form of Prakṛti's creative evolution)

## Related Mantras

- "Hrīm" (the bīja of Prakṛti-Śakti)
- Devī Māhātmya (the primary text of Prakṛti as the Goddess)

## Related Vidyās

## Traditional Interpretations

- Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti is one of the two ultimate realities — the material cause of the universe
- Advaita: Prakṛti = Māyā — the creative power of Brahman
- Viśiṣṭādvaita: Prakṛti is the body of Brahman-Viṣṇu — real and dependent
- Śākta: Prakṛti = the Goddess — the supreme creative reality

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Prakṛti as the natural world
- Upaniṣadic period: Prakṛti as the creative power of Brahman
- Sāṃkhya period (pre-classical): Prakṛti systematized as the material cause
- Classical Sāṃkhya (Īśvarakṛṣṇa, ~4th century CE): Prakṛti fully systematized
- Bhagavad Gītā: Prakṛti and Puruṣa integrated into the teaching of liberation
