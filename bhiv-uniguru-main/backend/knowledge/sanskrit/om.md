# Om

## Canonical Name

Om

## Sanskrit

ॐ

## Transliteration

Om (also Auṃ)

## Śabda

ॐ

## Dhātu

अव् (Av) — To protect, to pervade. Some traditions derive Om from the root av (to protect) or from the three letters A-U-M representing creation, preservation, dissolution.

## Vyākaraṇa

Om is classified as a praṇava (primordial sound) and an avyaya (indeclinable). It is not declined like ordinary nouns. Pāṇini treats it as a sacred syllable (maṅgala-vācaka). The written form ॐ is a ligature of the three letters A, U, M with the anusvāra (nasal resonance) and the bindu (point of silence above).

## Nirukta

Yāska: Om is the name of Brahman. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad: "Auṃ ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvam" — Om, this syllable is all this. The three letters represent the three states of consciousness (waking, dreaming, deep sleep) and the three worlds.

## Bīja

ॐ itself is the mahā-bīja (great seed syllable) — the source of all other bīja mantras.

## Tattva

Praṇava-Tattva — the principle of the primordial sound. Om is the sonic form of Brahman — the vibration from which the universe emerges and into which it dissolves.

## Śakti

Nāda-Śakti — the power of primordial sound. Om is the first vibration of Śakti — the creative power of the universe expressed as sound.

## Literal Meaning

The primordial sound; the syllable of Brahman; yes; so be it; the affirmation of existence.

## Functional Meaning

Om is the sonic representation of Brahman — the absolute reality expressed as vibration. It is used as a prefix to all mantras, prayers, and sacred texts. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad teaches that Om encompasses all of time (past, present, future) and all states of consciousness. Meditation on Om leads to the direct experience of Brahman.

## Ontology

In Advaita Vedānta: Om = Brahman; meditating on Om is meditating on Brahman. In Mīmāṃsā: Om is the eternal, self-existent sound (śabda-brahman) — the Vedas are the eternal sound of Brahman. In Tantra: Om is the mahā-bīja from which all other bīja mantras emerge. In Yoga: Om is the name of Īśvara (God) — "tasya vācakaḥ praṇavaḥ" (Yoga Sūtras 1.27).

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: Om is the sound of the universe — the cosmic vibration (nāda-brahman) from which all creation emerges. The Purāṇas describe Brahmā creating the universe by uttering Om. The concept of nāda (sound) as the first manifestation of Brahman underlies the cosmological significance of Om.

## Psychology

Meditation on Om (Om-japa, Om-dhyāna) is the primary practice for stilling the mind. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad maps the four parts of Om (A, U, M, silence) onto the four states of consciousness (waking, dreaming, deep sleep, turīya). The practice of Om-kāra-dhyāna (meditation on Om) leads through all four states to the recognition of Ātman = Brahman.

## Governance

Om as the foundation of governance: all royal proclamations and sacred oaths begin with Om. The concept of Om as the affirmation of cosmic order (ṛta) underlies its use in governance contexts.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Om is the primordial sound therapy. The vibration of Om is used in mantra-cikitsā (mantra therapy). Research suggests that chanting Om produces specific neurological effects — calming the nervous system and reducing stress.

## Mathematics

The concept of Om in mathematics: Om as the symbol of infinity (ananta) — the sound that contains all sounds. The bindu (point) above the Om symbol represents the mathematical point — dimensionless, yet the source of all dimensions.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Om is associated with the Sun (Sūrya) — the cosmic source of light and life. The Gāyatrī Mantra begins with Om and is addressed to the Sun. The concept of Om as the sound of the cosmic cycles (yugas, kalpas).

## Metallurgy

In ritual metallurgy: Om is inscribed on sacred objects to infuse them with divine power. The concept of Om as the mantra of consecration — all sacred metal objects are consecrated with Om.

## Ritual

Om begins and ends all Vedic rituals, mantras, and prayers. The Vedic tradition: "Om iti brahma" — Om is Brahman. The daily practice of Om-kāra-japa (repetition of Om) is the foundational spiritual practice. The concept of praṇava-upāsanā (worship through Om) is described in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad.

## Symbolism

The written symbol ॐ represents: A (waking state/creation), U (dreaming state/preservation), M (deep sleep/dissolution), the curve above (māyā/illusion), the dot (turīya/liberation), the silence after (Brahman). The three curves represent the three states of consciousness. The dot represents the fourth state (turīya) — pure awareness.

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Om appears as a sacred affirmation
- Upaniṣadic period: Om systematized as the symbol of Brahman (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad)
- Classical period: Om as the universal prefix to all sacred utterances
- Tantric period: Om as the mahā-bīja of all mantra science
- Modern: Om as the universal symbol of Indian spirituality
