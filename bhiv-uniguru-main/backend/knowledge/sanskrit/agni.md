# Agni

## Canonical Name

Agni

## Sanskrit

अग्नि

## Transliteration

Agni

## Śabda

अग्नि

## Dhātu

अग् (Ag) — To move tortuously; also connected to अञ्च् (Añc) — to go, to move. The root conveys the dynamic, moving nature of fire.

## Vyākaraṇa

Agni is a masculine noun, i-stem declension. One of the most frequently occurring words in the Ṛgveda (over 200 hymns addressed to Agni). Pāṇini treats Agni as a proper noun (deity name) and a common noun (fire).

## Nirukta

Yāska: "agni — agre nayati iti" — that which leads forward. Also: "agni — aṅgati iti" — that which moves. Fire is the leader, the first among the gods.

## Bīja

रं (Raṃ) — the bīja of fire (tejas) in Tantric traditions. Associated with the Maṇipūra chakra (solar plexus — the fire center).

## Tattva

Agni-Tattva — the fire principle. One of the five mahābhūtas (great elements): Pṛthvī (earth), Āp (water), Tejas/Agni (fire), Vāyu (air), Ākāśa (space). Agni is the transformative element — it converts one substance into another.

## Śakti

Tejas-Śakti — the power of radiance and transformation. Agni's śakti is the power to purify, transform, and illuminate. In Śākta traditions, Agni is the śakti of transformation (pariṇāma-śakti).

## Literal Meaning

Fire; the fire deity; the digestive fire; the cosmic transformative principle.

## Functional Meaning

Agni operates at multiple levels: (1) Physical fire — the element that transforms matter; (2) Jāṭharāgni — the digestive fire in the body; (3) Ritual fire — the medium of communication between humans and gods in yajña; (4) Cosmic fire — the transformative principle underlying all change; (5) Consciousness — Agni as the light of awareness (cit-agni).

## Ontology

In Vedic ontology: Agni is one of the three primary deities (Agni, Vāyu, Sūrya — fire, wind, sun). In Vaiśeṣika: Tejas (fire) is one of the nine dravyas (substances). In Sāṃkhya: Agni is the manifestation of the rajas guṇa — the active, transformative principle. In Tantra: Agni is the kuṇḍalinī-śakti — the fire that rises through the suṣumnā nāḍī.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: Agni is the messenger (dūta) between the human and divine realms — he carries the offerings of yajña to the gods. The three forms of Agni: Agni on earth (fire), Vāyu in the atmosphere (wind), Sūrya in the sky (sun). In Purāṇic cosmology: Agni is one of the Aṣṭadikpālas (guardians of the eight directions) — he guards the southeast.

## Psychology

Agni as psychological principle: the fire of discrimination (viveka-agni) burns away ignorance. The fire of desire (kāma-agni) is the psychological fire that must be transformed into the fire of knowledge (jñāna-agni). In Yoga psychology: the Maṇipūra chakra (fire center) governs will, power, and transformation.

## Governance

Agni in governance: the sacred fire (agni) is the witness of all royal ceremonies and oaths. The coronation ceremony (rājyābhiṣeka) involves the sacred fire. The concept of agni-parīkṣā (fire ordeal) as a test of truth in governance. The Arthaśāstra prescribes fire rituals for the protection of the kingdom.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Agni (digestive fire) is the central concept of health. The thirteen types of agni: jāṭharāgni (main digestive fire), five bhūtāgnis (elemental fires), seven dhātvāgnis (tissue fires). Health is balanced agni; disease is disturbed agni (sama, viṣama, tīkṣṇa, manda agni). The concept of āma (undigested toxins) arises from weak agni.

## Mathematics

In Vedic mathematics: the Agni-related altar constructions (agnicayana) required sophisticated geometry. The Śulbasūtras (altar-building manuals) contain the earliest known statements of the Pythagorean theorem, developed for constructing fire altars of precise areas.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Agni is associated with Mars (Maṅgala) — the fiery planet. The fire signs (Meṣa/Aries, Siṃha/Leo, Dhanus/Sagittarius) are governed by Agni's principle. The concept of agni-nakṣatra (fire stars) in Vedic astronomy.

## Metallurgy

Agni is the foundation of all metallurgy — fire transforms ore into metal. The Viśvakarmā tradition treats the smith's fire as sacred — a form of Agni. The concept of agni-karma (fire-work) in metallurgy: the proper use of fire according to the dharma of each metal. Smelting, forging, and casting are all forms of agni-yoga (union through fire).

## Ritual

Agni is the central deity of Vedic ritual. The five sacred fires (pañcāgni): Gārhapatya (household fire), Āhavanīya (offering fire), Dakṣiṇāgni (southern fire), Sabhya (assembly fire), Āvasathya (guest fire). The agnihotra (daily fire offering) is the foundational Vedic ritual. The agnicayana (fire altar construction) is the most elaborate Vedic ritual, taking 12 days.

## Symbolism

Fire symbolizes transformation, purification, and illumination. The flame pointing upward symbolizes the soul's aspiration toward the divine. The three flames of the sacred fire represent the three worlds (earth, atmosphere, sky). The eternal flame (akhaṇḍa-jyoti) symbolizes the undying nature of Ātman.

## Related Deities

- Indra (Agni's companion — together they receive the most Vedic hymns)

- Rudra (the fierce form of Agni)
- Skanda/Kārttikeya (born from Agni)

## Related Lokas

- Agni-loka (the realm of fire — southeast direction)
- Svarga (heaven — reached through the fire of yajña)

## Related Koshas

- Prāṇamaya Kosha (energy sheath) — Agni as prāṇa-śakti
- Annamaya Kosha (physical sheath) — Agni as jāṭharāgni (digestive fire)

## Related Chakras

- Maṇipūra (solar plexus chakra) — the fire chakra; seat of agni, will, and transformation
- Ājñā (third eye) — the fire of discrimination (viveka-agni)

## Related Yantras

- Agni Yantra (triangular — the triangle is the geometric symbol of fire)
- Śrī Yantra (contains the fire triangles pointing downward — Śakti's fire)

## Related Mantras

- "Agnim īḷe purohitam" (Ṛgveda 1.1.1) — the first verse of the Ṛgveda, addressed to Agni
- Agni Gāyatrī: "Om mahājvalāya vidmahe agnidevāya dhīmahi"
- "Svāhā" — the offering mantra used in all fire rituals

## Related Vidyās

- Agni-Vidyā (the science of fire — ritual and alchemical)
- Jyotiṣa Vidyā (fire signs and planets)
- Āyurveda (agni as the central concept of health)

## Related Śāstras

- Ṛgveda (Agni Maṇḍala — Book 1 and throughout)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Vedic: Agni is the divine messenger, the priest of the gods, the first among the immortals
- Āyurvedic: Agni is the principle of transformation and digestion — the basis of all health
- Tantric: Agni is the kuṇḍalinī-śakti — the fire of liberation
- Advaita: Agni is the fire of knowledge (jñāna-agni) that burns away ignorance

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Agni as the primary deity — the first word of the Ṛgveda
- Brāhmaṇa period: Agni as the ritual fire — elaborate fire ceremonies develop
- Upaniṣadic period: Agni as the inner fire of consciousness
- Āyurvedic period: Agni as the digestive and metabolic fire
- Tantric period: Agni as the kuṇḍalinī-śakti
