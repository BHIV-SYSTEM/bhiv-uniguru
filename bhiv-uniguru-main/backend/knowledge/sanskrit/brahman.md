# Brahman

## Canonical Name

Brahman

## Sanskrit

ब्रह्मन्

## Transliteration

Brahman

## Śabda

ब्रह्मन्

## Dhātu

बृह् (Bṛh) — To expand, to grow, to be great.

## Vyākaraṇa

Brahman is a neuter noun (not to be confused with Brahmā, the masculine creator deity, or Brāhmaṇa, the priestly class). Derived from the root bṛh with the suffix -man. The neuter gender indicates that Brahman transcends all categories including gender.

## Nirukta

Yāska: "bṛhati iti brahma" — that which is great, that which expands. Brahman is the infinitely expanding reality that underlies all existence.

## Bīja

ॐ (Om/Auṃ) — the primordial sound that is the sonic form of Brahman. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad opens: "Auṃ ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvam" — Om, this syllable is all this.

## Tattva

Brahma-Tattva — the principle of absolute, infinite, self-luminous reality. Brahman is described as Sat-Cit-Ānanda (Being-Consciousness-Bliss) — not three qualities but three aspects of one indivisible reality.

## Śakti

Brahman's śakti is Māyā — the creative power that projects the apparent multiplicity of the universe. In Śākta traditions, Brahman without Śakti is inert (śava); Brahman with Śakti is Śiva (the living absolute).

## Literal Meaning

The Absolute; the Infinite; the Ultimate Reality; the Ground of Being.

## Functional Meaning

Brahman is the infinite, eternal, unchanging absolute reality that is the ground of all existence. It is both the material cause (upādāna-kāraṇa) and the efficient cause (nimitta-kāraṇa) of the universe. Brahman is described via two approaches: Nirguṇa Brahman (without attributes — the absolute beyond all description) and Saguṇa Brahman (with attributes — the personal God, Īśvara). The Upaniṣads teach that Brahman is identical with Ātman (the individual Self).

## Ontology

In Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkara): Brahman alone is real (Brahma satyam); the world is appearance (jagat mithyā); the individual self is Brahman (jīvo brahmaiva nāparaḥ). In Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): Brahman is real, personal (Viṣṇu), and the universe and souls are Brahman's body. In Dvaita (Madhva): Brahman (Viṣṇu) is real and eternally distinct from souls and world. In Śaiva Siddhānta: Brahman is Śiva, the supreme reality with infinite śaktis.

## Cosmology

Brahman is the source, sustainer, and dissolution point of all universes. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad: "yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante" — from which all beings are born, by which they live, into which they return — that is Brahman. The Purāṇas describe Brahman as the ocean from which the universe arises like waves. The concept of Brahmaṇḍa (Brahman's egg) is the cosmic egg from which the universe hatches.

## Psychology

Brahman as psychological reality: the deepest layer of consciousness is Brahman. In Yoga psychology, the state of samādhi (absorption) is the direct experience of Brahman. The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad identifies Brahman with the fourth state of consciousness (turīya) that underlies waking, dreaming, and deep sleep. The experience of Brahman is described as "neti neti" (not this, not this) — Brahman cannot be grasped by the mind but is the ground of the mind.

## Governance

The concept of Brahman in governance: the ideal ruler is one who has realized Brahman and governs from that realization. The Arthaśāstra's concept of the philosopher-king parallels the Upaniṣadic ideal of the Brahman-realized ruler. The concept of Brahma-rāṣṭra (the kingdom of Brahman) appears in Vedic literature as the ideal polity.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Brahman is the ultimate source of health (ārogya). The concept of Brahma-cakra (the cycle of Brahman) underlies the Āyurvedic understanding of the body as a microcosm of the universe. The highest healing is Brahma-jñāna (knowledge of Brahman) which removes the root cause of all disease — avidyā (ignorance).

## Mathematics

The concept of Brahman in mathematics: Brahman as the infinite (ananta) is the philosophical basis for the Indian mathematical concept of infinity. The concept of śūnya (zero) as the void from which all numbers emerge mirrors the Brahman-as-source concept. The Upaniṣadic statement "pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idam" (that is full, this is full) is a mathematical statement about the nature of infinity.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Brahman is identified with the cosmic space (ākāśa) in which all celestial bodies move. The concept of Brahma-muhūrta (the hour of Brahman — 1.5 hours before sunrise) is the most auspicious time for spiritual practice. The Milky Way (Ākāśagaṅgā) is considered the visible form of Brahman's cosmic body.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: Brahman is the philosophical gold (parama-suvarṇa) — the ultimate substance that all metals aspire to become. The alchemical process of transmutation mirrors the spiritual process of realizing Brahman beneath the apparent multiplicity of substances.

## Ritual

The Brahma-yajña (sacrifice to Brahman) is the daily recitation of the Vedas — the highest ritual act. The Upanayana ceremony initiates the student into the study of Brahman. The Mahāvākyas are transmitted in ritual contexts as the direct teaching of Brahman. The concept of Brahma-vidyā (knowledge of Brahman) is the ultimate goal of all Vedic ritual.

## Symbolism

Om (ॐ) is the primary symbol of Brahman — the sound of the universe. The infinite space (ākāśa) symbolizes Brahman's all-pervading nature. The ocean symbolizes Brahman — waves (individual beings) arise from and return to the ocean. The sun symbolizes Nirguṇa Brahman — self-luminous, the source of all light. The bindu (point) in the center of a yantra represents Brahman as the source of all manifestation.

## Related Deities

- Brahman is not a deity but the ground of all deities
- Brahma (the creator) is Saguṇa Brahman's creative aspect
- Viṣṇu (the preserver) is Saguṇa Brahman's sustaining aspect
- Śiva (the destroyer) is Saguṇa Brahman's dissolving aspect
- Devī (the Goddess) is Brahman's Śakti — the dynamic aspect of the absolute

## Related Lokas

- Brahmaloka (Satyaloka) — the highest realm, closest to Brahman
- All lokas are within Brahman — "sarvam khalv idaṃ brahma" (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.1)

## Related Koshas

- All five koshas are within Brahman
- Ānandamaya Kosha (bliss sheath) is the closest approximation to Brahman in the individual
- Beyond all koshas is Ātman = Brahman

## Related Chakras

- Sahasrāra (crown chakra) — the seat of Brahman realization in the individual body
- The entire chakra system is within Brahman

## Related Yantras

- Śrī Yantra (the geometric representation of Brahman's creative expansion)
- Bindu (the central point — Brahman as the source)

## Related Mantras

- "Aum" (the sonic form of Brahman)
- "Aham Brahmāsmi" (I am Brahman — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10)
- "Prajñānam Brahma" (Consciousness is Brahman — Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.3)
- "Sarvam khalv idaṃ brahma" (All this is indeed Brahman — Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.1)

## Related Vidyās

- Brahmavidyā (knowledge of Brahman — the supreme vidyā)
- Ātmavidyā (self-knowledge = Brahmavidyā)
- Upaniṣad Vidyā (the teaching of the Upaniṣads)

## Related Śāstras

- Upaniṣads (primary source — especially Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya, Taittirīya, Māṇḍūkya)
- Brahma Sūtras (Bādarāyaṇa — systematic exposition of Brahman)
- Bhagavad Gītā (Chapter 13 — Kṣetra-Kṣetrajña; Chapter 14 — Guṇatraya Vibhāga)
- Vivekacūḍāmaṇi (Śaṅkara)
- Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya (Śaṅkara's commentary)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkara): Brahman alone is real; world and individual self are appearances in Brahman
- Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): Brahman is personal (Viṣṇu); world and souls are Brahman's body
- Dvaita (Madhva): Brahman (Viṣṇu) is real and eternally distinct from souls and world
- Śaiva Siddhānta: Brahman is Śiva with infinite śaktis
- Śākta: Brahman is the Goddess (Devī) — the dynamic absolute
- Kashmir Śaivism: Brahman is Śiva-Śakti — pure consciousness and its creative power

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Brahman primarily means "sacred utterance" or "prayer power"
- Atharvaveda: Brahman as cosmic power
- Brāhmaṇa period: Brahman as the power of ritual
- Early Upaniṣads: Brahman as the ultimate reality, identical with Ātman
- Brahma Sūtras (200 BCE–200 CE): Systematic philosophy of Brahman
- Śaṅkara (8th century CE): Advaita — Brahman as the only reality
- Rāmānuja (11th century CE): Viśiṣṭādvaita — Brahman as personal God
- Madhva (13th century CE): Dvaita — Brahman as Viṣṇu, eternally distinct
