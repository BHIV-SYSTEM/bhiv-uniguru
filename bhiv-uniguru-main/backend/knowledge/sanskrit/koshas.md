# Koshas

## Canonical Name

Koshas

## Sanskrit

कोशाः

## Transliteration

Kośāḥ

## Śabda

कोश (Kośa)

## Dhātu

कुश् (Kuś) — To enclose, cover, sheath, contain.

## Vyākaraṇa

Derived from verbal root कुश् (kuś). Masculine noun, plural form कोशाः (Kośāḥ). Pāṇini Ashtadhyayi 3.1.134. Refers to the five sheaths enclosing the Ātman.

## Nirukta

Yāska's Nirukta: "kuśnāti ātmānam iti kośaḥ" — that which covers or sheaths the innermost Self. Like a scabbard covering a sword, the koshas veil the luminous Ātman.

## Bīja

हँ (Haṁ) — the subtle vāyu seed syllable associated with sheath transmutations.

## Tattva

Pañca-Kośa Tattva — the fivefold doctrine of human embodiment detailed in the Taittirīya Upanishad.

## Śakti

Kośāvaraṇa-Śakti — the veiling power of Māyā acting through physical, energetic, mental, intellectual, and bliss sheaths.

## Literal Meaning

Sheaths, coverings, vessels, inner containers.

## Functional Meaning

The Koshas represent the five concentric sheaths or layers of human existence: Annamaya Kosha (food/physical sheath), Prāṇamaya Kosha (vital energetic sheath), Manomaya Kosha (mental/emotional sheath), Vijñānamaya Kosha (wisdom/intellectual sheath), and Ānandamaya Kosha (bliss sheath). They provide a systematic model for self-realization by peeling away identification with non-self layers.

## Ontology

In Taittirīya Upanishad: The koshas form a hierarchy of subtlety, each inner kosha filling and animating the outer one. In Advaita Vedānta: The koshas belong to the anatman (non-Self); realization occurs through Pañca-Kośa-Viveka (discrimination between Self and the five sheaths).

## Cosmology

The five koshas correlate with the macrocosmic elements (Pañca-Mahābhūta) and cosmic bodies: Gross body (sthūla-śarīra = Annamaya), Subtle body (sūkṣma-śarīra = Prāṇamaya, Manomaya, Vijñānamaya), and Causal body (kāraṇa-śarīra = Ānandamaya).

## Psychology

In Yogic and Vedāntic psychology: Mental afflictions (kleśas), saṃskāras, and vasanās reside primarily within the Manomaya and Vijñānamaya koshas. Purification of each kosha systematically stabilizes mental clarity.

## Governance

Dharmic governance addresses human welfare across all five koshas: physical security (Annamaya), vital health (Prāṇamaya), emotional peace (Manomaya), intellectual education (Vijñānamaya), and spiritual flourishing (Ānandamaya).

## Medicine

Āyurveda treats disease (vyādhi) according to kosha origin: physical ailments originate in Annamaya, energetic imbalances in Prāṇamaya, psycho-somatic disorders (ādhi) in Manomaya. Holistic healing requires balancing all sheaths.

## Engineering

Vāstu Śāstra applies kosha principles to building layout, structuring space from outer protective boundaries (Annamaya equivalent) to the central sacred precinct (Brahmasthāna / Ānandamaya core).

## Mathematics

The nesting ratio of the five koshas mirrors divine geometry and golden ratio proportions found in traditional temple architecture and yantra design.

## Astronomy

Kosha dynamics correspond to celestial influences: Sun governs Prāṇamaya, Moon governs Manomaya, Jupiter governs Vijñānamaya, reflecting cosmic energetic sheaths.

## Metallurgy

Rasashāstra compares the purification of mercury (Sūta) through eighteen samskāras to the progressive refinement of the human koshas.

## Ritual

Ritual practices target specific koshas: Āsana for Annamaya, Prāṇāyāma for Prāṇamaya, Japa/Mantra for Manomaya, Svādhyāya for Vijñānamaya, Dhyāna for Ānandamaya.

## Symbolism

Symbolized by concentric golden rings, nested wooden boxes, or the layers of an unpeeled lotus flower revealing the central jewel (mani).

## Traditional Interpretations

- Śaṅkarācārya: The koshas are false attributions (adhyāsa) on Ātman; negating them via "neti neti" reveals the unattached Witness (Sākṣī).
- Vidyāraṇya Svāmī: Pañcadaśī Chapter 3 details the method of isolating Ātman from the five sheaths.
- Abhinavagupta: Koshas are joyful self-expressions (krīḍā) of divine consciousness.

## Historical Evolution

Introduced canonically in Taittirīya Upanishad, elaborated in classical Advaita Vedānta treatises, and integrated into Hatha and Tantric Yoga practices.
