# Shakti

## Canonical Name

Shakti

## Sanskrit

शक्ति

## Transliteration

Śakti

## Śabda

शक्ति

## Dhātu

शक् (Śak) — To be able, to have power, to be capable.

## Vyākaraṇa

Śakti is a feminine noun, i-stem declension. Derived from the root śak (to be able) with the suffix -ti. The feminine gender is significant — power in the Indian tradition is inherently feminine.

## Nirukta

"Śaknoti iti śaktiḥ" — that which is capable, that which has power. Śakti is both the power itself and the Goddess who embodies that power.

## Bīja

ह्रीं (Hrīm) — the primary bīja of Śakti. Also क्रीं (Krīm) — the bīja of Kālī-Śakti; ऐं (Aim) — the bīja of Sarasvatī-Śakti; श्रीं (Śrīm) — the bīja of Lakṣmī-Śakti.

## Tattva

Śakti-Tattva — the principle of divine power and creative energy. In Śākta philosophy: Śakti is the ultimate reality — Brahman as dynamic power. In Śaiva philosophy: Śakti is Śiva's inseparable power — "Śivaḥ śaktyā yukto yadi bhavati śaktaḥ prabhavitum" (Śiva united with Śakti is capable of creating — Soundaryalaharī 1).

## Śakti

Śakti is itself the principle of śakti — it is self-referential. The three primary śaktis: Icchā-Śakti (will-power), Jñāna-Śakti (knowledge-power), Kriyā-Śakti (action-power).

## Literal Meaning

Power; energy; capability; the Goddess; the divine feminine principle.

## Functional Meaning

Śakti is the dynamic, creative, and liberating power of the universe. It is the feminine principle that activates the masculine principle (Śiva/Puruṣa). Without Śakti, Śiva is śava (a corpse) — inert. Śakti manifests as the three primary powers: Icchā (will), Jñāna (knowledge), and Kriyā (action). In the individual, Śakti manifests as kuṇḍalinī — the dormant power at the base of the spine that, when awakened, leads to liberation.

## Ontology

In Śākta philosophy: Śakti is the ultimate reality — Brahman as dynamic power. The Goddess (Devī) is not a secondary deity but the supreme reality. In Kashmir Śaivism: Śakti is Śiva's inseparable power — the universe is Śiva-Śakti's dance. In Advaita: Śakti is Brahman's māyā-śakti — the creative power of the absolute. In Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti is the equivalent of Śakti — the primal creative power.

## Cosmology

In Śākta cosmology: the universe is the Goddess's body — she is both the creator and the creation. The Devī Māhātmya describes Śakti as the power that creates, preserves, and dissolves the universe. The concept of Śakti-pīṭhas (power seats) — 51 or 108 sacred sites where the Goddess's power is concentrated.

## Psychology

Śakti as psychological principle: the three śaktis (icchā, jñāna, kriyā) correspond to will, intellect, and action in the individual. Kuṇḍalinī-śakti is the psychological-spiritual power that, when awakened through yoga, transforms consciousness. The concept of śakti-pāta (descent of grace) — the Guru's transmission of śakti to the student.

## Governance

The concept of Śakti in governance: the ruler's power (rāja-śakti) is a manifestation of divine Śakti. The Arthaśāstra describes three types of royal power: prabhāva-śakti (power of majesty), mantra-śakti (power of counsel), utsāha-śakti (power of energy). The concept of rāṣṭra-śakti (national power) in Indian political philosophy.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Śakti is the vital power (ojas) — the refined essence of all bodily tissues. The concept of śakti in Āyurvedic therapy: building ojas (vital power) through proper diet, lifestyle, and herbs. The concept of śakti-vardhana (power enhancement) in Āyurvedic medicine.

## Mathematics

The concept of Śakti in mathematics: the power function (śakti) in Indian mathematics — the concept of raising a number to a power. The Bījaganita (algebra) of Bhāskara uses śakti in the sense of "square" (the power of a number).

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Śakti is associated with the Moon (Candra) and Venus (Śukra) — the feminine planets. The concept of graha-śakti (planetary power) — each planet has its own śakti. The nakṣatra Śakti is associated with specific astrological powers.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: Śakti is the feminine principle in alchemy — sulfur (gandhaka) is the Śakti to mercury's (pārada) Śiva. The union of mercury and sulfur is the alchemical Śiva-Śakti union. The concept of dhātu-śakti (power of metals) — each metal has its own śakti.

## Ritual

The Śakti-pūjā (worship of the Goddess) is the primary Śākta ritual. The Navārātra (nine nights of the Goddess) is the primary Śākta festival. The concept of śakti-upāsanā (worship of power) — using the Goddess's power for liberation. The Tantric ritual of śakti-sādhana (practice with the Goddess's power).

## Symbolism

The triangle (trikona) pointing downward symbolizes Śakti — the feminine, receptive, creative power. The lotus symbolizes Śakti's creative power — blooming from the waters of consciousness. The lion (siṃha) is Śakti's vehicle — representing her power over the ego. The trident (triśūla) represents Śakti's three powers (icchā, jñāna, kriyā).

## Related Deities

- Devī/Mahādevī (the supreme Goddess — Śakti herself)
- Durgā (Śakti as the warrior Goddess)
- Kālī (Śakti as the power of time and liberation)
- Lakṣmī (Śakti as the power of abundance)
- Sarasvatī (Śakti as the power of knowledge)
- Śiva (Śakti's inseparable consort)

## Related Lokas

- Śakti-loka (the realm of the Goddess)
- All lokas are within Śakti — she is the universe

## Related Koshas

- All five koshas are manifestations of Śakti
- Prāṇamaya Kosha (energy sheath) — the most direct manifestation of Śakti in the individual

## Related Chakras

- Mūlādhāra (root chakra) — the seat of dormant kuṇḍalinī-śakti
- All seven chakras are centers of Śakti's manifestation
- Sahasrāra (crown) — the seat of Śiva-Śakti union

## Traditional Interpretations

- Śākta: Śakti is the supreme reality — the Goddess is the ultimate Brahman
- Kashmir Śaivism: Śakti is Śiva's inseparable power — the universe is their dance
- Advaita: Śakti is Brahman's māyā-śakti — the creative power of the absolute
- Vaiṣṇava: Śakti is Viṣṇu's power (Lakṣmī, Bhū, Nīlā)

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Śakti as the power of the gods (Indra's śakti, Varuṇa's śakti)
- Upaniṣadic period: Śakti as Brahman's creative power
- Purāṇic period: Śakti as the Goddess — Devī Māhātmya (6th century CE)
- Tantric period (500–1200 CE): Śakti as the supreme reality — Śākta Tantra develops
- Kashmir Śaivism (9th–12th century CE): Śakti as Śiva's inseparable power
- Modern: Śakti as a concept in feminist spirituality and consciousness studies
