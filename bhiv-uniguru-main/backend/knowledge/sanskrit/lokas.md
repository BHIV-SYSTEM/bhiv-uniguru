# Lokas

## Canonical Name

Lokas

## Sanskrit

लोकाः

## Transliteration

Lokāḥ

## Śabda

लोक (Loka)

## Dhātu

लोक् (Lok) — To see, perceive, experience, shine.

## Vyākaraṇa

Derived from verbal root लोक् (lok) with the ghañ suffix. Masculine noun, plural form लोकाः (Lokāḥ). Pāṇini Ashtadhyayi 3.3.18. Signifies realms of perception and manifestation.

## Nirukta

Yāska's Nirukta: "lokyate ity lokaḥ" — that which is perceived or experienced. A loka is not merely a geographic place, but a dimensional field of consciousness and experience.

## Bīja

लँ (Laṁ) — the earth seed syllable associated with manifestation across realms.

## Tattva

Loka-Tattva — the structural hierarchy of dimensional existence within Prakṛti, inhabited by jīvas according to karma and consciousness state.

## Śakti

Lokādhāra-Śakti — the sustaining power of the Divine Mother (Bhuvaneśvarī) holding the seven upper and seven lower worlds in cosmic balance.

## Literal Meaning

Perceived worlds, fields of experience, dimensional realms.

## Functional Meaning

Lokas represent the multi-dimensional structure of the cosmos in Indian cosmology. They classify states of manifestation and consciousness ranging from physical density to subtle divine awareness. In Vedāntic and Purāṇic frameworks, lokas serve as fields of karmic fruition (bhoga-bhūmi).

## Ontology

In Sāṃkhya-Yoga: Lokas are progressive combinations of the three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) forming subtle and gross planes of experience. In Advaita Vedānta: Lokas exist within the vyāvahārika (relative) order of reality, superimposed upon Brahman by Māyā.

## Cosmology

Purāṇic and Vedic cosmology details fourteen Lokas: seven upper worlds (Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ, Mahaḥ, Janaḥ, Tapaḥ, Satyaloka/Brahmaloka) and seven lower realms (Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Rasātala, Talātala, Mahātala, Pātāla). Satyaloka represents the highest plane of truth.

## Psychology

In Yoga psychology: Lokas correspond to internal states of awareness and meditative absorption (dhāraṇā and samādhi). Transition between lokas reflects the refinement of the subtle body (sūkṣma-śarīra) and purification of the mind (citta).

## Governance

Loka-saṃgraha (the protection and maintenance of world order) is a core precept of Bhagavad Gītā (3.20). Dharmic governance aims at maintaining harmony across the lokas.

## Medicine

Āyurveda views the human body (piṇḍāṇḍa) as a microcosm of the fourteen lokas (brahmāṇḍa). Health requires alignment between internal bio-energies and cosmic loka cycles.

## Engineering

Vāstu Śāstra organizes architectural spaces according to the grid of directional devatās presiding over the different lokas, harmonizing structural forms with cosmic dimensions.

## Mathematics

Vedic cosmology utilizes exponential units of measurement (yojanas) and temporal cycles (yugas, manvantaras, kalpas) to describe the scale and duration of the lokas.

## Astronomy

Jyotiṣa maps celestial spheres and planetary orbits (graha-mandala) as physical reflections of the subtle lokas, connecting astronomical positions with cosmic realms.

## Metallurgy

Rasashāstra associates specific metals with planetary devatās presiding over different lokas (e.g., Gold with Satyaloka/Sūrya, Iron with Saturn/lower realms).

## Ritual

Vedic yajñas invoke the three primary lokas (Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ) through the Vyāhṛtis during ritual oblations, restoring systemic resonance between human and divine planes.

## Symbolism

Symbolized by the cosmic tree (Aśvattha) with roots above and branches below, or by the multi-layered lotus (Padma) blooming across dimensions.

## Traditional Interpretations

- Śaṅkarācārya: Lokas are relative fields of experience experienced by the jīva based on karma; ultimately sublated by self-knowledge.
- Rāmānujācārya: Lokas are real divine realms created by Viṣṇu for the enjoyment and liberation of souls.
- Abhinavagupta: Lokas are stages of divine self-expansion (ābhāsa) of Paramaśiva.

## Historical Evolution

From early Vedic tri-loka (Bhū, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ) in Rigveda to the fully articulated 14-loka schema of the Epics and Purāṇas, evolving into psychological planes in Tantric and Yogic syntheses.
