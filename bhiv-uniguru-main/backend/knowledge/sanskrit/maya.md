# Maya

## Canonical Name

Maya

## Sanskrit

माया

## Transliteration

Māyā

## Śabda

माया

## Dhātu

मा (Mā) — To measure, to create, to construct. Māyā is "that which measures out" or "that which creates the appearance of measure and form."

## Vyākaraṇa

Māyā is a feminine noun. Derived from the root mā (to measure/create) with the suffix -yā. In Vedic usage, māyā means "supernatural power" or "creative magic." In Advaita Vedānta, it acquires the technical meaning of "cosmic illusion."

## Nirukta

"Māyate iti māyā" — that which measures/creates. Also: "mā — not, yā — that which is" — that which is not (the appearance of what is not real). The second etymology is a later philosophical interpretation.

## Bīja

ह्रीं (Hrīm) — the bīja of Māyā-Śakti in Tantric traditions. Hrīm is the bīja of the Goddess as the creative power of illusion and liberation.

## Tattva

Māyā-Tattva — the principle of creative illusion and cosmic appearance. In Advaita: Māyā is the power of Brahman that projects the apparent multiplicity of the universe. In Kashmir Śaivism: Māyā is one of the five kañcukas (coverings) that limit the universal consciousness.

## Śakti

Māyā-Śakti — the creative and concealing power. Māyā has two functions: āvaraṇa-śakti (concealing power — it conceals Brahman) and vikṣepa-śakti (projecting power — it projects the apparent world).

## Literal Meaning

Illusion; creative power; supernatural power; the cosmic appearance.

## Functional Meaning

Māyā is the cosmic power that creates the appearance of multiplicity in the non-dual Brahman. It is neither real (sat) nor unreal (asat) — it is anirvachanīya (indescribable). Māyā operates through avidyā (ignorance) at the individual level and through the cosmic creative power at the universal level. Liberation (mokṣa) is the transcendence of māyā through jñāna (knowledge).

## Ontology

In Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkara): Māyā is the power of Brahman that creates the appearance of the world. It is neither real nor unreal — it is mithyā (apparent). The world is not non-existent (it appears) but not ultimately real (it is not Brahman). In Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): Māyā is Viṣṇu's creative power — real, not illusory. In Dvaita (Madhva): Māyā is Viṣṇu's power — the world created by māyā is real. In Kashmir Śaivism: Māyā is one of the thirty-six tattvas — a real power of Śiva that limits universal consciousness.

## Cosmology

In Purāṇic cosmology: Māyā is Viṣṇu's creative power (Yoga-Māyā) that maintains the cosmic illusion. The Devī Māhātmya describes Māyā as the Goddess herself — the cosmic creative power. In Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti (primal matter) is the equivalent of Māyā — the creative power that produces the universe for Puruṣa's experience.

## Psychology

Māyā as psychological principle: avidyā (individual ignorance) is the psychological form of cosmic māyā. The three psychological functions of māyā: āvaraṇa (concealment of the true Self), vikṣepa (projection of the false self), and mala (impurity). Liberation requires the removal of māyā through viveka (discrimination) and vairāgya (dispassion).

## Governance

The concept of māyā in governance: the ruler who is deluded by māyā governs for personal gain rather than dharma. The Arthaśāstra warns against the māyā of power — the illusion that the king is the ultimate authority. The concept of rāja-māyā (royal illusion) — the danger of rulers believing their own propaganda.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: māyā (illusion/delusion) is a cause of mental disease (mānasa-roga). The concept of prajñāparādha (crime against wisdom) — acting against one's better knowledge — is the Āyurvedic equivalent of māyā's concealing function.

## Mathematics

The concept of māyā in mathematics: the appearance of multiplicity from unity mirrors the mathematical concept of differentiation. The concept of māyā as "measurement" (from the root mā) connects to the mathematical act of measuring — imposing finite measure on the infinite.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Māyā is associated with the Moon (Candra) — the planet of illusion, reflection, and the mind. The Moon's phases represent māyā's changing appearances. The concept of graha-māyā (planetary illusion) — the apparent retrograde motion of planets is a form of māyā.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: māyā is the appearance of base metals as gold — the alchemist's task is to see through māyā to the true nature of substances. The concept of māyā in metallurgy: the surface appearance of a metal (its color, luster) may conceal its true nature.

## Ritual

The concept of māyā in ritual: ritual itself operates within māyā — it uses the apparent world to point beyond it. The concept of māyā-pūjā (worship through the apparent) — using forms, images, and rituals to transcend form. The Tantric tradition explicitly works with māyā — using the apparent world as the path to liberation.

## Symbolism

The veil symbolizes māyā — it conceals what is behind it. The mirror symbolizes māyā — it creates an apparent reflection that is not the original. The dream symbolizes māyā — vivid and real while experienced, but recognized as unreal upon waking. The magician (māyāvī) symbolizes māyā's creative power.

## Related Deities

## Related Lokas

- All lokas are within māyā — they are māyā's projections
- Brahmaloka is beyond māyā

## Related Koshas

- Sahasrāra (crown) — beyond māyā

- Devī Māhātmya (Māyā as the Goddess)
- Yoga Vāsiṣṭha (māyā and liberation)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Advaita Vedānta: Māyā is the cosmic illusion — neither real nor unreal; transcended by jñāna
- Viśiṣṭādvaita: Māyā is Viṣṇu's real creative power — the world is real
- Dvaita: Māyā is Viṣṇu's power — the world created by māyā is real
- Śākta: Māyā is the Goddess herself — the creative power of the universe
- Kashmir Śaivism: Māyā is one of the thirty-six tattvas — a real power of Śiva

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Māyā as supernatural creative power (of Varuṇa, Indra)
- Upaniṣadic period: Māyā as the creative power of Brahman
- Gauḍapāda (7th century CE): Māyā as the basis of the apparent world
- Śaṅkara (8th century CE): Māyā as cosmic illusion — the central concept of Advaita
- Rāmānuja (11th century CE): Critique of Śaṅkara's māyā — the world is real
- Modern: Māyā as a concept in comparative philosophy and consciousness studies
