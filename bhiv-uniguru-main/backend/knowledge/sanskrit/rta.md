# Rta

## Canonical Name

Rta

## Sanskrit

ऋत

## Transliteration

Ṛta

## Śabda

ऋत

## Dhātu

ऋ (Ṛ) — To go, to move, to flow. Ṛta is "that which has moved/flowed in the right way" — the right order of things.

## Vyākaraṇa

Ṛta is a neuter noun derived from the past passive participle of the root ṛ (to go/flow). It means "that which has gone rightly" — the right order, the truth, the cosmic law.

## Nirukta

Yāska: "ṛtam — satyam" — Ṛta is truth. The cosmic truth that underlies all existence. Ṛta is both the cosmic order and the truth that expresses it.

## Bīja

ऋ (Ṛ) — the vowel ṛ itself is the bīja of cosmic order and right movement.

## Tattva

Ṛta-Tattva — the principle of cosmic truth and right order. Ṛta is the Vedic precursor to Dharma — the cosmic law that governs all existence before it was articulated as ethical principle.

## Śakti

Ṛta-Śakti — the power of cosmic truth. The śakti that maintains the right order of the universe — the movements of the sun, the seasons, the moral order of human society.

## Literal Meaning

Cosmic order; truth; right; the law of the universe.

## Functional Meaning

Ṛta is the Vedic concept of cosmic truth and right order. It governs the movements of the celestial bodies, the cycle of seasons, the moral order of human society, and the efficacy of ritual. Varuṇa is the primary guardian of Ṛta — he punishes those who violate it. Ṛta is the foundation from which Dharma (ethical order) later developed. The concept of Ṛta underlies the Vedic understanding of truth (satya) — to speak truth is to align with Ṛta.

## Ontology

In Vedic ontology: Ṛta is the cosmic law that precedes and underlies all existence. It is not created by the gods but is the principle by which the gods themselves operate. In later philosophy: Ṛta becomes Dharma (ethical order) and Satya (truth) — the two primary expressions of cosmic law.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: Ṛta governs the movements of the sun (Sūrya follows Ṛta), the cycle of seasons (ṛtu — season — derives from Ṛta), the flow of rivers, and the moral order of the universe. The concept of Ṛta-dhāman (the abode of Ṛta) — the cosmic realm where Ṛta is perfectly expressed.

## Psychology

Ṛta as psychological principle: the person who lives in alignment with Ṛta experiences inner harmony. The violation of Ṛta (anṛta — untruth, adharma) creates psychological disturbance. The concept of ṛta-cit (Ṛta-consciousness) — the mind that perceives and aligns with cosmic truth.

## Governance

Ṛta as the foundation of governance: the king who governs according to Ṛta maintains cosmic order. The concept of rāja-dharma (royal duty) is the application of Ṛta to governance. Varuṇa's role as the guardian of Ṛta is the model for the king's role as the guardian of dharma.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Ṛta as the right order of the body — health is the body's alignment with Ṛta. The concept of ṛtu-caryā (seasonal regimen) — living according to the seasonal Ṛta for optimal health.

## Mathematics

The concept of Ṛta in mathematics: mathematical truth (satya) is a form of Ṛta — the right order of numbers and relationships. The Vedic concept of mathematical precision in ritual (Śulbasūtras) is an expression of Ṛta.

## Astronomy

Ṛta is most directly expressed in astronomy — the right movements of celestial bodies. The concept of ṛtu (season) derives from Ṛta — the seasons are the cosmic Ṛta expressed in time. Vedic astronomy (Jyotiṣa) is the science of tracking Ṛta in the sky.

## Metallurgy

The concept of Ṛta in metallurgy: the right order of metallurgical processes — following the dharma of each metal is following its Ṛta.

## Ritual

Yajña (sacrifice) is the primary ritual expression of Ṛta — it maintains and reinforces cosmic order. The concept of ṛta-yajña (sacrifice according to Ṛta) — ritual performed in alignment with cosmic truth. The Vedic concept of ṛtvij (the priest who performs the ritual at the right time — ṛtu + ij) — the priest who aligns ritual with Ṛta.

## Symbolism

The sun symbolizes Ṛta — it follows its ordained path without deviation. The river symbolizes Ṛta — it flows in its right course. The seasons symbolize Ṛta — they follow their right order.

## Related Deities

- Varuṇa (the primary guardian of Ṛta — he punishes violations of cosmic order)
- Mitra (Varuṇa's companion — together they uphold Ṛta)
- Sūrya (the sun — the most visible expression of Ṛta in the cosmos)
- Agni (the fire — the ritual medium of Ṛta)

## Related Lokas

- Ṛta-dhāman (the abode of Ṛta — the cosmic realm of perfect order)

## Related Koshas

- Vijñānamaya Kosha (intellect sheath) — the seat of ṛta-cit (Ṛta-consciousness)

## Related Chakras

- Ājñā (third eye) — the seat of perception of cosmic order

## Related Yantras

- Varuṇa Yantra (for aligning with cosmic order)

## Related Mantras

- Varuṇa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 7.86-89 — hymns to the guardian of Ṛta)
- "Ṛtam vada, satyam vada" (Speak Ṛta, speak truth)

## Related Vidyās

- Ṛta Vidyā (the science of cosmic order — the foundation of all Vedic knowledge)
- Jyotiṣa Vidyā (the science of tracking Ṛta in the sky)

## Related Śāstras

- Ṛgveda (Ṛta appears throughout — especially in Varuṇa hymns)
- Atharvaveda (Ṛta as cosmic truth)
- Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.1 ("Ṛtam vada, satyam vada")

## Traditional Interpretations

- Vedic: Ṛta is the cosmic law that governs all existence — the foundation of dharma
- Later Vedānta: Ṛta becomes Satya (truth) and Dharma (ethical order)
- Comparative: Ṛta is related to Iranian Asha (truth/order) and possibly Greek Themis

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Ṛta as the primary cosmic principle — the most important concept in the Ṛgveda
- Atharvaveda: Ṛta begins to be replaced by Dharma
- Upaniṣadic period: Ṛta becomes Satya (truth) — "satyam eva jayate" (truth alone triumphs)
- Post-Vedic: Dharma replaces Ṛta as the primary ethical-cosmic concept
