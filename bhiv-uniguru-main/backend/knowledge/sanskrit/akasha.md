# Akasha

## Canonical Name

Akasha

## Sanskrit

आकाश

## Transliteration

Ākāśa

## Śabda

आकाश

## Dhātu

आ + काश् (Ā + Kāś) — Ā (towards, completely) + Kāś (to shine, to be luminous). That which shines completely; the luminous space.

## Vyākaraṇa

Ākāśa is a masculine noun derived from the prefix ā- and the root kāś (to shine). The word conveys both the sense of "space" and "luminosity" — space is not empty but luminous.

## Nirukta

"Ākāśate iti ākāśaḥ" — that which shines. Space is not mere emptiness but the luminous ground of all existence.

## Bīja

हं (Haṃ) — the bīja of the space element (ākāśa) in Tantric traditions. Associated with the Viśuddha chakra (throat — the space center).

## Tattva

Ākāśa-Tattva — the space principle. The first and subtlest of the five mahābhūtas (great elements): Ākāśa (space), Vāyu (air), Agni (fire), Āp (water), Pṛthvī (earth). Ākāśa is the substratum of all other elements — they all exist within space.

## Śakti

Ākāśa-Śakti — the power of infinite space. The śakti that provides the ground for all manifestation. In Tantric traditions, ākāśa is the śakti of pure potentiality.

## Literal Meaning

Space; ether; sky; the luminous void.

## Functional Meaning

Ākāśa is the subtlest of the five elements — the space in which all other elements and all phenomena exist. It is the medium of sound (śabda) — sound travels through ākāśa. In Vedānta, ākāśa is the first element to emerge from Brahman in the process of creation. The Chāndogya Upaniṣad teaches that Brahman is ākāśa — the infinite space of consciousness.

## Ontology

In Vaiśeṣika: Ākāśa is one of the nine dravyas (substances) — it is eternal, all-pervading, and the substratum of sound. In Advaita: Ākāśa is the first manifestation of Brahman — "ākāśo ha vai nāma nāmarūpayor nirvahitā" (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.14.1). In Sāṃkhya: Ākāśa is the first tanmātra (subtle element) — śabda-tanmātra (sound-essence) — from which the gross space element emerges.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: Ākāśa is the first element created — from Brahman comes ākāśa, from ākāśa comes vāyu, from vāyu comes agni, from agni comes āp, from āp comes pṛthvī (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1). The concept of cidākāśa (consciousness-space) — the infinite space of pure awareness that is Brahman.

## Psychology

Ākāśa as psychological principle: the mind (manas) is like ākāśa — it provides the space in which thoughts arise and dissolve. The concept of cidākāśa (consciousness-space) — the pure awareness that is the ground of all mental activity. The practice of ākāśa-dhāraṇā (concentration on space) leads to the experience of infinite consciousness.

## Governance

The concept of ākāśa in governance: the ruler who governs from the space of ākāśa (non-attachment, equanimity) governs wisely. The concept of rāja-ākāśa (the space of the king) — the king's court as the space where all voices are heard.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Ākāśa is the element associated with the vāta doṣa (along with vāyu). The concept of ākāśa in the body: the spaces within the body (channels, cavities) are the body's ākāśa. Ākāśa imbalance leads to vāta disorders — anxiety, dryness, instability.

## Mathematics

The concept of ākāśa in mathematics: the concept of infinite space (ananta-ākāśa) is the philosophical basis for the Indian mathematical concept of infinity. The concept of śūnya (zero) as the mathematical ākāśa — the empty space from which all numbers emerge.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Ākāśa is the space in which all celestial bodies move. The concept of ākāśa-gaṅgā (the Milky Way — the river of space). The nakṣatra system maps the ākāśa into 27 divisions.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: ākāśa is the space in which alchemical transformations occur. The concept of ākāśa as the medium of subtle influences — the alchemist works with the ākāśa of the laboratory.

## Ritual

The concept of ākāśa in ritual: the ritual space (yajña-śālā) is a consecrated ākāśa — a space set apart from ordinary space. The concept of maṇḍala (sacred circle) as a bounded ākāśa. The concept of ākāśa-dīpa (sky lamp) — a lamp offered to the infinite space.

## Symbolism

The sky (ākāśa) symbolizes infinite space, freedom, and the divine. The blue color symbolizes ākāśa — the sky's color. The void (śūnya) symbolizes ākāśa's nature as the ground of all existence. The mirror symbolizes ākāśa — it reflects all without being affected.

## Related Deities

- Viṣṇu (Vaikuṇṭha — Viṣṇu's realm is the highest ākāśa)
- Śiva (Cidambaram — the space of consciousness — Śiva's primary form is ākāśa)
- Indra (the lord of the sky — the cosmic ākāśa)

## Related Lokas

- All lokas exist within ākāśa
- Brahmaloka (the highest ākāśa — the space of Brahman)

## Related Koshas

- All five koshas exist within ākāśa
- Ānandamaya Kosha (bliss sheath) — the subtlest kosha, closest to ākāśa

## Related Chakras

- Viśuddha (throat chakra) — the ākāśa chakra; the seat of space and sound
- Sahasrāra (crown chakra) — the infinite ākāśa of pure consciousness

## Related Yantras

- Ākāśa Yantra (circular — the circle represents infinite space)

## Related Mantras

- "Haṃ" (the bīja of ākāśa)
- "Sarvam khalv idaṃ brahma" (All this is Brahman — the ākāśa of Brahman)

## Related Vidyās

- Ākāśa Vidyā (the science of space — Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.14)
- Cidākāśa Vidyā (the science of consciousness-space)

## Related Śāstras

- Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1 (ākāśa as the first element)
- Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.14 (Brahman as ākāśa)
- Vaiśeṣika Sūtras (ākāśa as a substance)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Vaiśeṣika: Ākāśa is an eternal, all-pervading substance — the substratum of sound
- Advaita: Ākāśa is the first manifestation of Brahman; cidākāśa (consciousness-space) is Brahman itself
- Sāṃkhya: Ākāśa emerges from the śabda-tanmātra (sound-essence)

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Ākāśa as the sky and cosmic space
- Upaniṣadic period: Ākāśa as the first element and as Brahman
- Vaiśeṣika period: Ākāśa systematized as one of nine substances
- Tantric period: Ākāśa as the space of consciousness (cidākāśa)
