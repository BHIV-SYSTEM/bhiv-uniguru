# Chakras

## Canonical Name

Chakras

## Sanskrit

चक्राणि

## Transliteration

Cakrāṇi

## Śabda

चक्र (Cakra)

## Dhātu

क्रम् (Kram) / चक् (Cak) — To move, rotate, turn, shine.

## Vyākaraṇa

Derived from verbal root क्रम् / चक् with reduplication and suffix -ra. Neuter noun, plural form चक्राणि (Cakrāṇi). Pāṇini Ashtadhyayi 3.1.114. Refers to subtle energy centers or vortices.

## Nirukta

Yāska's Nirukta: "cakram cakanaṁ vā, kramaṇam vā, krayam vā" — that which revolves or moves forward continuously. Chakras are psycho-spiritual centers of energy flow along the central channel (Suṣumnā nāḍī).

## Bīja

लँ, वँ, रँ, यँ, हँ, ॐ (Laṁ, Vaṁ, Raṁ, Yaṁ, Haṁ, Om) — the root seed syllables for the six major chakras plus Sahasrāra.

## Tattva

Cakra-Tattva — the subtle energy centers regulating the transformation of prāṇa and tattvas within the subtle body (sūkṣma-śarīra).

## Śakti

Kuṇḍalinī-Śakti — the dormant spiritual evolutionary force coiled at Mūlādhāra chakra, which pierces the six chakras (Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Bheda) to unite with Śiva at Sahasrāra.

## Literal Meaning

Wheels, vortices, dynamic circles, energy hubs.

## Functional Meaning

Chakras are primary psycho-energetic nodes located along the central spinal axis where nāḍīs (energy channels) intersect. They govern physical organ systems, emotional patterns, mental states, and spiritual awakening stages. The primary seven chakras are Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Maṇipūra, Anāhata, Viśuddha, Ājñā, and Sahasrāra.

## Ontology

In Tantra and Hatha Yoga: Chakras are real energetic structures composed of subtle matter (sūkṣma-tattva). In Śāktism: They are divine lotus thrones (padma) of presiding deities and śaktis within the body.

## Cosmology

The seven chakras correspond macrocosmically to the seven upper lokas (Bhūḥ to Satyaloka) and the seven cosmic elements (Pañca-Mahābhūta + Manas + Buddhi).

## Psychology

Each chakra represents a psychological stage of human evolution: survival (Mūlādhāra), sexuality/creativity (Svādhiṣṭhāna), power/will (Maṇipūra), love/compassion (Anāhata), communication/truth (Viśuddha), intuition/wisdom (Ājñā), and divine unity (Sahasrāra).

## Governance

Chakra balances inform self-governance and leadership ethics (Rājadharma): leaders operating from heart (Anāhata) and wisdom (Ājñā) chakras govern with dharma and compassion.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda and Siddha medicine: Chakras correspond to major nerve plexuses (e.g., sacral, solar plexus, cardiac, carotid) and endocrine glands (thyroid, pituitary, pineal). Imbalances manifest as physical disease.

## Engineering

Bio-energetic architecture in Vāstu builds spaces aligning with the body's chakra axis, balancing thermal, magnetic, and acoustic resonances.

## Mathematics

Each chakra features a precise number of lotus petals (4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 2, 1000), total 50 petals corresponding to the 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet (Mātṛkā-varṇa).

## Astronomy

Chakra energies align with planetary presences: Saturn (Mūlādhāra), Jupiter (Svādhiṣṭhāna), Mars (Maṇipūra), Venus (Anāhata), Mercury (Viśuddha), Sun/Moon (Ājñā).

## Metallurgy

Rasashāstra links specific metallic ashes (bhasmas) to individual chakra activations (e.g., Suvarṇa bhasma with Sahasrāra, Loha bhasma with Maṇipūra).

## Ritual

Nyāsa (ritual placement of mantras and touch upon body locations) systematically activates and sanctifies the chakras prior to worship or meditation.

## Symbolism

Symbolized by multi-petaled lotuses containing geometric yantras, seed syllables, animal mounts, and presiding male-female deity pairs.

## Traditional Interpretations

- Svāmī Pūrṇānanda (Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa): Detailed description of petal counts, colors, yantras, and awakening methodology for each chakra.
- Abhinavagupta (Tantrāloka): Chakras are centers of consciousness transformation where the divine energy (śakti) elevates from individual to universal awareness.

## Historical Evolution

Found implicitly in Vedic Upanishads (Katha, Śvetāśvatara), systematized in Tantric texts (6th-10th century CE), and popularized globally in 20th-century Yoga integration.
