# Yajna

## Canonical Name

Yajna

## Sanskrit

यज्ञ

## Transliteration

Yajña

## Śabda

यज्ञ

## Dhātu

यज् (Yaj) — To worship, to sacrifice, to offer.

## Vyākaraṇa

Yajña is a masculine noun derived from the root yaj (to worship/sacrifice) with the suffix -na. The root yaj is one of the most important roots in Vedic Sanskrit — it gives yajña (sacrifice), yajus (sacrificial formula), yajamāna (sacrificer), and Yajurveda (the Veda of sacrificial formulas).

## Nirukta

Yāska: "yajyate devā anena iti yajñaḥ" — that by which the gods are worshipped. Yajña is the act of offering that creates and maintains the relationship between humans and the divine.

## Bīja

ॐ (Om) — the primordial offering. Also स्वाहा (Svāhā) — the mantra of offering in fire rituals.

## Tattva

Yajña-Tattva — the principle of sacred exchange and cosmic reciprocity. The universe itself is a yajña — a continuous cycle of giving and receiving between all levels of existence.

## Śakti

Yajña-Śakti — the power of sacred offering. The śakti that maintains the cosmic order through the cycle of giving.

## Literal Meaning

Sacrifice; offering; worship; the sacred act of giving.

## Functional Meaning

Yajña is the foundational principle of Vedic civilization — the sacred act of offering that maintains cosmic order (ṛta). The Bhagavad Gītā (3.10-16) describes yajña as the cosmic cycle: Brahmā created beings along with yajña; through yajña, rain falls; from rain, food grows; from food, beings live; beings perform yajña — the wheel of cosmic reciprocity. Yajña operates at five levels: Brahma-yajña (study of Vedas), Deva-yajña (fire offerings to gods), Pitṛ-yajña (offerings to ancestors), Bhūta-yajña (offerings to all beings), Manuṣya-yajña (hospitality to humans).

## Ontology

In Mīmāṃsā: yajña is the primary means of achieving all human goals — dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa. The apūrva (unseen potency) of yajña persists until it bears fruit. In Advaita: yajña at the highest level is the offering of the ego into the fire of knowledge (jñāna-yajña). In the Bhagavad Gītā: karma-yoga is yajña — all action performed without attachment is an offering to the divine.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: the universe itself was created through a cosmic yajña — the Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.90) describes the primordial sacrifice of the cosmic Puruṣa from which all creation emerged. The concept of yajña as the mechanism of cosmic maintenance — the gods depend on yajña for their sustenance.

## Psychology

Yajña as psychological principle: the offering of the ego (ahaṃkāra) into the fire of self-knowledge is the highest yajña. The Bhagavad Gītā (4.24-33) describes multiple forms of yajña as psychological practices: offering the senses into the fire of restraint, offering actions into the fire of knowledge.

## Governance

Rāja-sūya (royal consecration) and Aśvamedha (horse sacrifice) are the primary royal yajñas. The king's yajña maintains the prosperity of the kingdom. The concept of rāṣṭra-yajña (national sacrifice) — governance as a form of yajña for the welfare of all.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: yajña smoke (dhūpa) is used for purification and healing. The concept of yajña as environmental medicine — fire offerings purify the atmosphere. Modern research on yajña smoke (homa therapy) suggests antimicrobial properties.

## Mathematics

The Śulbasūtras (altar-building manuals) contain the earliest known mathematical theorems, developed for constructing yajña altars of precise areas. The Pythagorean theorem, geometric transformations, and irrational numbers appear in the context of yajña altar construction.

## Astronomy

The timing of yajñas is determined by Jyotiṣa (Vedic astronomy). The concept of muhūrta (auspicious time) for yajña is based on astronomical calculations. The Nakṣatra system was developed partly for determining the correct times for yajña.

## Metallurgy

The sacred fire (agni) of yajña is the basis of all metallurgical fire. The concept of dhātu-yajña (offering of metals) — specific metals are offered in specific yajñas for specific results.

## Ritual

The five mahāyajñas (great sacrifices): Brahma-yajña, Deva-yajña, Pitṛ-yajña, Bhūta-yajña, Manuṣya-yajña. The Soma yajña (Agniṣṭoma, Vājapeya, Rājasūya) are the major Vedic sacrifices. The agnihotra (daily fire offering) is the minimal yajña obligation.

## Symbolism

The fire symbolizes yajña — the transformative medium of offering. The smoke rising from the fire symbolizes the offering reaching the divine realm. The svāhā (offering mantra) symbolizes the act of giving without reservation.

## Related Deities

- Agni (the fire deity — the medium of yajña)
- Indra (the primary recipient of Soma offerings)
- Viṣṇu (Yajña is one of Viṣṇu's names — he is the cosmic sacrifice)
- Brahmā (the creator who established yajña)

## Related Lokas

- Svarga (heaven — the destination of yajña's merit)
- Pitṛloka (the realm of ancestors — maintained by Pitṛ-yajña)

## Related Koshas

- Prāṇamaya Kosha (energy sheath) — the field of prāṇa-yajña (offering of breath)
- Annamaya Kosha (physical sheath) — the field of physical offerings

## Related Chakras

- Maṇipūra (solar plexus) — the fire chakra; the seat of the inner yajña
- Anāhata (heart chakra) — the seat of bhakti-yajña (devotional offering)

## Related Yantras

- Agni Yantra (the triangular yantra of the fire offering)

## Related Mantras

- "Svāhā" (the offering mantra)
- Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.90 — the cosmic sacrifice)
- Gāyatrī Mantra (the mantra of the solar yajña)

## Related Vidyās

## Related Śāstras

## Traditional Interpretations

- Mīmāṃsā: Yajña is the primary means of achieving all human goals
- Advaita: Jñāna-yajña (offering of knowledge) is the highest yajña
- Bhagavad Gītā: All action performed without attachment is yajña

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Yajña as the primary religious act
- Brāhmaṇa period: Elaborate yajña rituals developed
- Upaniṣadic period: Yajña internalized as psychological practice
- Bhagavad Gītā: Yajña as the cosmic principle of selfless action
- Modern: Yajña as environmental ritual and community practice
