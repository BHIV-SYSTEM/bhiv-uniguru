# Guru

## Canonical Name

Guru

## Sanskrit

गुरु

## Transliteration

Guru

## Śabda

गुरु

## Dhātu

गृ (Gṛ) — To swallow, to take in; also connected to गुर् (Gur) — heavy, weighty. The guru is the "heavy one" — weighty with knowledge.

## Vyākaraṇa

Guru is an adjective (meaning "heavy, weighty, important") used as a noun. It is also used as a title. The feminine form is Gurū or Gurviṇī. The word is used in both the sense of "teacher" and "heavy/important."

## Nirukta

"Gu-kāraś cāndhakārasya ru-kāras tannirodhakṛt" — Gu represents darkness (ignorance), Ru represents its removal. The Guru is one who removes the darkness of ignorance. This etymology appears in the Advayatāraka Upaniṣad.

## Bīja

गुं (Guṃ) — the bīja associated with the Guru principle in Tantric traditions.

## Tattva

Guru-Tattva — the principle of the teacher who transmits knowledge and removes ignorance. In Kashmir Śaivism: Guru-tattva is one of the thirty-six tattvas — the principle of divine grace operating through the human teacher.

## Śakti

Guru-Śakti — the power of the teacher to transmit knowledge and grace. The concept of śakti-pāta (descent of grace) — the Guru's transmission of spiritual power to the student.

## Literal Meaning

Heavy; weighty; important; teacher; spiritual master.

## Functional Meaning

The Guru is the teacher who transmits knowledge (vidyā) from the tradition to the student. In the Vedic tradition, knowledge cannot be obtained from books alone — it must be transmitted from Guru to student in an unbroken lineage (paramparā). The Guru removes the student's ignorance (avidyā) and guides them toward liberation (mokṣa). The Guru-śiṣya (teacher-student) relationship is the foundational relationship of Indian civilization.

## Ontology

In Advaita Vedānta: the Guru is the external manifestation of the inner Guru (Ātman). The Guru's teaching points the student to their own true nature. In Tantra: the Guru is the living embodiment of the divine — "Guru is Brahma, Guru is Viṣṇu, Guru is Maheśvara" (Guru Gītā). In Kashmir Śaivism: the Guru is the agent of Śiva's grace — śakti-pāta operates through the Guru.

## Cosmology

In Purāṇic cosmology: Bṛhaspati is the Guru of the gods (deva-guru); Śukra is the Guru of the demons (asura-guru). The concept of Ādi-Guru (the first Guru) — Śiva is the Ādi-Guru who taught yoga to Pārvatī. The concept of Guru-paramparā (lineage of teachers) — the unbroken chain from the divine source to the present teacher.

## Psychology

The Guru-śiṣya relationship as psychological transformation: the Guru mirrors the student's unconscious patterns and guides their dissolution. The concept of guru-bhakti (devotion to the Guru) as a psychological practice — surrendering the ego to the Guru's guidance. The concept of guru-kṛpā (Guru's grace) — the transformative power of the Guru's attention and blessing.

## Governance

The concept of Guru in governance: the rāja-guru (royal teacher) was the king's spiritual and political advisor. Cāṇakya (Kauṭilya) was the paradigmatic rāja-guru — he guided Candragupta Maurya to establish the Maurya Empire. The concept of dharma-guru (teacher of righteousness) — the Guru who guides the ruler in dharmic governance.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: the Guru is the teacher of medical knowledge. The Guru-śiṣya relationship is the primary mode of Āyurvedic transmission. The concept of guru-upadeśa (Guru's instruction) as the most important element of Āyurvedic education. The Guru's direct observation and correction of the student's practice is essential.

## Mathematics

The concept of Guru in mathematics: the transmission of mathematical knowledge through the Guru-śiṣya relationship. The Āryabhaṭīya and other mathematical texts were transmitted through Guru-paramparā. The concept of guru-mukha-vidyā (knowledge from the Guru's mouth) — mathematical knowledge that cannot be obtained from texts alone.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Guru (Jupiter) is the planet of wisdom, teaching, and expansion. Jupiter is the Guru of the solar system — the largest planet, the teacher of the other planets. The concept of guru-bala (Jupiter's strength) in the birth chart — a strong Jupiter indicates wisdom and good teachers.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: the Guru is the master alchemist who transmits the secret knowledge of Rasaśāstra. The concept of guru-dīkṣā (initiation by the Guru) in alchemy — the student cannot practice alchemy without the Guru's initiation and guidance.

## Ritual

The Guru-pūjā (worship of the Guru) is performed on Guru Pūrṇimā (the full moon of Āṣāḍha). The Guru-vandanā (salutation to the Guru) begins all learning. The concept of guru-dakṣiṇā (gift to the Guru) — the student's offering to the Guru upon completion of study. The Upanayana saṃskāra initiates the student into the Guru-śiṣya relationship.

## Symbolism

The lamp (dīpa) symbolizes the Guru — the Guru's knowledge illuminates the student's darkness. The sun symbolizes the Guru — the source of light and life. The river symbolizes the Guru's teaching — it flows from the source (tradition) to nourish the student.

## Traditional Interpretations

- Advaita: The Guru is the external manifestation of the inner Guru (Ātman)
- Tantra: The Guru is the living embodiment of the divine — Guru = Brahman
- Vaiṣṇava: The Guru is the representative of Viṣṇu — serving the Guru is serving God
- Kashmir Śaivism: The Guru is the agent of Śiva's grace

## Historical Evolution

- Vedic period: The Guru as the teacher of the Vedas in the gurukula
- Upaniṣadic period: The Guru as the transmitter of Brahmavidyā
- Classical period: The Guru-śiṣya relationship systematized
- Tantric period: The Guru as the initiator and transmitter of śakti
- Modern: The Guru concept challenged and reinterpreted
