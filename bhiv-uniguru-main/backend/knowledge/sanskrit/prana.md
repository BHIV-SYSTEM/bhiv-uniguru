# Prana

## Canonical Name

Prana

## Sanskrit

प्राण

## Transliteration

Prāṇa

## Śabda

प्राण

## Dhātu

प्र + अन् (Pra + An) — Pra (forward, forth) + An (to breathe, to live). That which breathes forth; the forward-moving breath.

## Vyākaraṇa

Prāṇa is a masculine noun. The prefix pra- (forward) combined with the root an- (to breathe) gives prāṇa — the primary, forward-moving life-breath. Distinguished from the five vāyus: Prāṇa (inward breath), Apāna (downward breath), Samāna (equalizing breath), Udāna (upward breath), Vyāna (pervading breath).

## Nirukta

"Prāṇiti iti prāṇaḥ" — that which breathes is prāṇa. The life-force that animates all living beings.

## Bīja

यं (Yaṃ) — the bīja of vāyu (air/wind), the element most closely associated with prāṇa. Also हं (Haṃ) — the bīja of the throat chakra (Viśuddha) associated with prāṇa's vocal expression.

## Tattva

Prāṇa-Tattva — the life-force principle. Prāṇa is the bridge between the physical body (annamaya kosha) and the mind (manomaya kosha). It is the animating principle that distinguishes the living from the dead.

## Śakti

Prāṇa-Śakti — the vital power. The śakti that animates all living beings. In Śākta traditions, prāṇa-śakti is the Goddess's life-giving power manifest in every breath.

## Literal Meaning

Breath; life-force; vital energy; the animating principle.

## Functional Meaning

Prāṇa is the universal life-force that animates all living beings. It operates through the prāṇamaya kosha (energy sheath) and the network of nāḍīs (energy channels). The five prāṇas (pañca-vāyu) govern specific physiological functions: Prāṇa (respiration, heart), Apāna (elimination, reproduction), Samāna (digestion, assimilation), Udāna (speech, upward movement, death), Vyāna (circulation, distribution). Prāṇāyāma (breath control) is the primary yogic practice for working with prāṇa.

## Ontology

In Vedic ontology: Prāṇa is the cosmic life-force — "prāṇo vai brahma" (Prāṇa is indeed Brahman — Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad). In Sāṃkhya: Prāṇa is the activity of the subtle body (sūkṣma-śarīra). In Advaita: Prāṇa is the manifestation of Brahman as the life-force. In Āyurveda: Prāṇa is the primary vāta-doṣa — the governing principle of all movement and nervous function.

## Cosmology

In Vedic cosmology: Prāṇa is the cosmic breath — the universe breathes in and out through cosmic cycles (kalpa). The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad describes a cosmic debate among the prāṇas to determine which is supreme — prāṇa (breath) wins because when it leaves, all other faculties cease. In Purāṇic cosmology: Prāṇa is the breath of Brahma — the universe is created on his outbreath and dissolved on his inbreath.

## Psychology

In Yoga psychology: prāṇa and mind (manas) are intimately connected — "prāṇe manaḥ" (the mind is in prāṇa). Controlling prāṇa through prāṇāyāma controls the mind. The concept of prāṇa-spanda (the vibration of prāṇa) in Kashmir Śaivism is the basis of all mental activity. The five prāṇas govern different psychological functions: Prāṇa (attention, perception), Apāna (letting go, release), Samāna (integration, balance), Udāna (aspiration, will), Vyāna (expansion, connection).

## Governance

The concept of prāṇa in governance: the king's prāṇa (vitality) is the prāṇa of the kingdom. The Arthaśāstra prescribes prāṇāyāma and yoga for the ruler's mental clarity. The concept of rāṣṭra-prāṇa (the life-force of the nation) appears in political philosophy.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: Prāṇa is the primary vāta-doṣa governing the nervous system, respiration, and all movement. Prāṇa-vāyu specifically governs the heart and respiratory system. Prāṇa imbalance is the root of all vāta disorders. Prāṇāyāma is the primary Āyurvedic therapy for prāṇa disorders. The concept of prāṇa-pratīkāra (prāṇa therapy) includes breath work, marma therapy, and yoga.

## Mathematics

The concept of prāṇa in mathematics: the Vedic concept of prāṇa as the animating principle of numbers — each number has its own prāṇa (vitality). The concept of prāṇa in Vedic meter (chandas): each meter has a specific prāṇa (breath pattern) that gives it its power.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: Prāṇa is associated with the Sun (Sūrya) — the cosmic prāṇa-source. The concept of prāṇa-āyuṣa (life-span calculation) in Jyotiṣa uses prāṇa as the unit of time. The prāṇa (a unit of time = 4 seconds) is the smallest unit in Vedic time measurement.

## Metallurgy

In alchemical traditions: prāṇa is the life-force that must be infused into alchemical preparations. The concept of prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā (installation of life-force) in idol-making is the ritual infusion of prāṇa into a metal image. Mercury (pārada) is considered the prāṇa of metals in Rasaśāstra.

## Ritual

Prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā (installation of life-force) is the ritual that animates a deity's image. The prāṇāyāma performed before ritual purifies the prāṇa of the practitioner. The concept of prāṇa-āhuti (offering of prāṇa) in yajña — the breath itself is offered to the fire.

## Symbolism

The breath symbolizes prāṇa — the most immediate experience of life-force. The wind (vāyu) symbolizes prāṇa's invisible, pervading nature. The serpent (nāga) symbolizes prāṇa's movement through the nāḍīs. The lotus symbolizes prāṇa's blooming in the heart.

## Related Deities

- Vāyu (the wind deity — the cosmic form of prāṇa)
- Indra (the lord of prāṇa in the Upaniṣads)
- Hanumān (the son of Vāyu — the embodiment of prāṇa-śakti)
- Prāṇa-devī (the Goddess as life-force)

## Related Lokas

- Vāyu-loka (the realm of wind/prāṇa)
- All lokas are sustained by prāṇa

## Related Koshas

- Prāṇamaya Kosha (energy sheath) — the primary domain of prāṇa
- Annamaya Kosha (physical sheath) — animated by prāṇa
- Manomaya Kosha (mind sheath) — connected to prāṇa through breath

## Related Chakras

- Anāhata (heart chakra) — the seat of prāṇa-vāyu
- Viśuddha (throat chakra) — the seat of udāna-vāyu
- Svādhiṣṭhāna (sacral chakra) — the seat of apāna-vāyu
- Maṇipūra (solar plexus) — the seat of samāna-vāyu

## Related Yantras

- Vāyu Yantra (hexagonal — the geometric form of the air element)
- Prāṇa Yantra (for prāṇa healing practices)

## Related Mantras

- "So'ham" (I am That) — the natural mantra of the breath (So = inhalation, Ham = exhalation)
- "Prāṇāya svāhā" (offering to prāṇa — from the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad)
- Prāṇa Gāyatrī: "Om prāṇāya vidmahe vāyudevāya dhīmahi"

## Related Vidyās

- Prāṇāyāma Vidyā (science of breath control)
- Prāṇa-Vidyā (science of life-force — Upaniṣadic teaching)
- Marma Vidyā (science of vital points — prāṇa junctions in the body)

## Related Śāstras

- Praśna Upaniṣad (dedicated to the teaching of prāṇa)
- Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 6.1 (the debate of the prāṇas)
- Yoga Sūtras 2.49-53 (prāṇāyāma)
- Caraka Saṃhitā (prāṇa in Āyurveda)
- Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (prāṇāyāma techniques)

## Traditional Interpretations

- Vedic: Prāṇa is Brahman — the cosmic life-force
- Āyurvedic: Prāṇa is the primary vāta-doṣa — the governing principle of all movement
- Yogic: Prāṇa is the bridge between body and mind — controlling prāṇa controls the mind
- Tantric: Prāṇa is the vehicle of consciousness — where prāṇa goes, consciousness follows

## Historical Evolution

- Ṛgvedic period: Prāṇa as breath and life
- Upaniṣadic period: Prāṇa as cosmic life-force and Brahman
- Āyurvedic period: Prāṇa systematized as the five vāyus
- Yogic period: Prāṇāyāma developed as a systematic practice
- Tantric period: Prāṇa as the vehicle of kuṇḍalinī-śakti
